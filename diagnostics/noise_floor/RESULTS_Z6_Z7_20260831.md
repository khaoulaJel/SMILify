# Z6 / Z7 — the registration is at its noise floor, and 8 of 71 morphometric traits survive it

Workers only. Job 3353787 (Z6 fits), Z7 zero additional compute.

## Z6 — ~0.60 is the floor, not a failure

25 species × 2 conspecific workers (mostly consecutive accessions, i.e. probably nest-mates), one
species per genus, determined species only. All four voiding checks passed — and **the population
spread matched `bench50_clean` to 1.00×**, so the denominator confound is excluded outright.

| | LOO (conspecific present) | leave-pair-out | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| composed shape | **0.6171** | 0.6276 | **+0.0105** |
| betas only | 0.2671 | 0.2807 | +0.0136 |

Giving the model a nest-mate buys **+0.0105** — less than the ~0.01 run-to-run spread Z5 measured.

Zero-model control, no model involved: within-pair distance ÷ spread = **1.3147**, against a null
of **√2 ≈ 1.414** for two independent draws. **Conspecifics are only 7% closer than random ants.**

**≈0.60 is the achievable target on real worker scans.** The instrument floor of 0.00 under exact
correspondence is not reachable, and measuring the residual against it is what misled this
investigation in the first place.

## Z7 — the goal question: which measurements can be trusted?

The workers exist for the morphometrics. Two nest-mates should measure the same. For each trait in
the shipped measurement layer (`diagnostics/morphometrics/measure.py`, including its Mosimann
log-shape-ratio step):

    R = 1 − σ²_within-pair / σ²_total

| | count |
|---|---:|
| **R ≥ 0.5 — usable** | **8** |
| 0.2 ≤ R < 0.5 — weak | 32 |
| **R < 0.2 — noise** | **31** (9 of them negative) |
| median R | **0.217** |

**The usable set:**

| trait | R |
|---|---:|
| `head_wid` | 0.691 |
| `mandible_r_len` | 0.613 |
| `shapedev_mesosoma` | 0.607 |
| `attach_an_1_l` | 0.568 |
| `shapedev_gaster` | 0.559 |
| `attach_an_1_r` | 0.546 |
| `seg_b_a_1` (petiole) | 0.538 |
| `shapedev_head` | 0.535 |

**What fails, and it is not random.** The casualties are overwhelmingly `seg_*` bone lengths on
legs and antennae — `seg_l_1_ti_r` −0.187, `seg_l_3_co_l` 0.056, `seg_l_2_ta_r` 0.016. Nine traits
are **negative**: two nest-mates disagree *more* than two random ants. These are exactly the distal
structures REPORT §4 identified as invisible to the area-sampled data term (tibia+tarsus+pretarsus
= 2.4% of a leg chain's area), and exactly the structures the entire W- and C-series tried and
failed to fix. Z7 says why that never mattered: **those measurements were never carrying signal.**

**A built-in internal check.** Bilateral pairs should have equal reliability. `attach_an_1_l/r`
read 0.568 / 0.546 — consistent, and credible. `mandible_r_len` 0.613 vs `mandible_l_len` 0.254 —
inconsistent, so the mandible length is not as trustworthy as its right-side number suggests. Any
trait whose left and right versions disagree should be treated as unreliable regardless of the
better side.

## What this means for the morphometrics report

**The aggregate genus result is not undermined.** 1-NN genus classification at 6.1× lift
(p < 0.0001) is multivariate — a classifier can extract real signal from many individually weak
features, and nothing here contradicts it.

**Per-trait biological claims need this table.** The cephalic-index claim (§4, *Odontomachus*
0.83 vs 0.98, p = 0.024) is head width ÷ head length: `head_wid` is the single most reliable trait
(R = 0.691), but `head_len` is weak (R = 0.271). The claim rests on a good numerator and a shaky
denominator and should be re-examined against this.

**Recommendation:** restrict per-trait biological claims to the R ≥ 0.5 set, or weight traits by R
in multivariate analyses. Leg and antenna segment lengths should not carry biological claims on
worker registrations.

## Where this leaves the investigation

Seven hypotheses for the worker gap are closed (Z1 stale anchor, X1 scale magnitude, Z2 rotation
freedom, Z3 joint blendshapes, Z4 free-form capacity, Z5 free-form asymmetry, Z6 the gap itself).
The registration sits at its noise floor. **The productive question is no longer how to improve the
fit — it is which measurements survive the noise, and Z7 answers that.**

The natural next step, and the first one in this series that is about the science rather than the
fitter: recompute the morphometrics report's headline results using only the R ≥ 0.5 traits, and
see which conclusions strengthen, which weaken, and which were resting on noise. That needs the
full 757-worker corpus, and the repeatability table should be rebuilt there too — 25 pairs gives a
usable ranking but a coarse estimate of each R.
