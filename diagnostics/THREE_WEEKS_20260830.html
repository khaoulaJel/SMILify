Branch synthesis · diagnostics/moonshot/REPORT.md

The pipeline was scoring well by destroying the thing registration exists to produce

Observation

Surface metrics on the stock ants_cfg.yaml looked fine. Per-stage instrumentation of the actual parameter deltas did not: stages 2 and 3 run scheme: 'deform', and the measured change in joint_rot over those 2000 iterations was exactly 0.00000.

StageItersΔ joint_rotΔ betasΔ deform
Stage_0_init2000.310.000.00
Stage_1_pose_shape2001.420.830.00
Stage_2_deform_coarse10000.000000.000004.87
Stage_3_deform_fine10000.000000.000002.11
Theory

83% of the compute cannot change a joint angle. Whatever pose error survives iteration 400 is permanent, and the rest of the budget is spent hiding it with per-vertex offsets. If that is what is happening, the damage should be visible in template-relative quantities the chamfer score never touches.

Measured

Mean edge distortion vs. template 74%. Local vertex neighbourhoods destroyed 36%. Free-form offset magnitude 2× the best arm's. The pipeline produces surface fits, not registrations.

Confirmed independently: a leave-one-out shape model built from these registrations reconstructs a held-out specimen no better than the population mean (gen/spread 0.99–1.00), while the same metric reaches 0.42 at 10 modes on synthetic data with exact correspondence. No amount of extra corpus fixes this.

Also found
  • Pose is trapped, not under-optimised. Raising the pose learning rate produced a result worse than its own initialisation — six substitutable legs make a multimodal objective, and bigger steps move you between basins, not down one.
  • Two real code defects: the edge regulariser compared against the original template (penalising legitimate pose change), and target points were sampled per-vertex rather than per-area (letting dense regions dominate the data term).
  • scaledirs/transdirs are never used by the fitter at all. Shape was living in a pose-space parameter. Wiring them in did not fix the shape freeze, but the defect is real.
  • Outlier rejection is contraindicated. Robust kernels delete thin structure: distal legs are read as outliers at 36× the body's rate.
  • Mesh preprocessing is not the blocker. Cleaning worker meshes to reference quality on 6 of 7 axes made the scoring target easier and the registration marginally worse.
  • Fittability is predictable from the scan alone. radial_med predicts fit quality at ρ 0.80–0.81, validated on held-out specimens (deform_mag +38.9%, p = 4.5e-13).
A mistake worth naming

An earlier draft listed "betas_prec is computed and never used" as a defect and activated it as w_beta_prior. It is not a defect — it is legacy SMAL machinery the SMIL workflow deliberately does not use. That term then closed the shape space in every arm built on the hierarchical pipeline (betas sd 0.00038 vs. 0.068–0.50 elsewhere, a 100–1000× split invisible to every surface metric). Removing it produced the only movement in the correspondence metric the whole investigation had achieved. The whole cost traces to inferring intent from absence.

Two things ship out of this thread: M7_handoff_midline (chamfer +60.68 ± 0.34% over 3 seeds, from a four-line midsagittal planarity penalty on the 223 sym_verts) and the one-word free win scheme: 'deform' → 'all'. And one framing that governs everything after it: every per-part metric in this suite is source→target proximity, and shrink-wrapping games it.

Nine attempts at penetration, and three confirmed reasons it is genuinely hard

Observation

A fitted mesh can look accurate from outside — good chamfer, good silhouette — while a leg passes straight through the gaster. Turning on w_penetration reliably makes existing contact shallower but not less frequent. That count/severity split is what opened the thread.

9Post-hoc repair (SIGGRAPH 2025 method)Whole-mesh F-score −0.152; the mechanistically-motivated refinement made it worse (−0.326, one specimen collapsed)
Why it's hard

Three separate mechanisms, each independently evidenced rather than guessed:

  • Near-tie matching instability. Any hard threshold on a continuous geometric quantity has vertices sitting at the boundary that flip between otherwise-identical runs. Flipped vertices sit 6–7× closer to the boundary than stable ones in 7/10 specimens. This affects the count metric itself, whichever loss produced the fit.
  • Rough gradients from discrete primitives. GWN's gradient near its own boundary is known-rough in the literature, and TASK6 measured exactly that signature.
  • A repeated-invocation fault in the one well-conditioned primitive. The BVH kernel crashes when called in any loop — confirmed caller-agnostic across a training step and a single-energy repair loop. Isolated calls work every time. A third-party implementation bug, not a conceptual problem.

Nothing ships as a penetration fix. Two things ship anyway: penetration_soft_num_penetrating is now the standard comparison metric (the hard count has ~40% seed-to-seed CV; the soft count cuts that 25–45%), and symmetric_chamfer_sampling ships as an opt-in surface-quality win independent of the penetration question.

Turning the moonshot findings into one recipe that actually runs

Theory

The moonshot branch produced a pile of individually-validated levers. A production recipe needs each one re-tested in combination, on the corpus that ships, with a bar set before the run — because levers that help alone can interact badly.

Ported
  • scheme: all — pose never frozen during deform
  • symmetric area-weighted chamfer sampling on both meshes
  • w_offset penalty on deform_verts
  • w_midline anatomical prior on sym_verts
  • rest_edge_loss + edge_mode toggle (default unchanged)
  • authored joint limits, via PR #97/#98 merged to master
Decided
  • w_offset 5.0/2.0 confirmed near-optimal — full sweep across 0.2/0.08 → 200/200, non-monotonic as predicted.
  • edge_mode: rest REJECTED — tested in combination with the winning offset, correctness collapsed 6.69% → 1.52%. Interacts negatively; not merely redundant.
  • scale_cap (w_scale = 0.052) SHIPPED after a pre-registered 838-specimen A/B: genus classification non-inferior (11.4% vs 11.7% LOO-1NN), integrity shift <1% on every term, anterior joint-scale outlier tail down 58–70%.
  • A silent config defect caught and fixed: w_limit: 0.006 was absent from D1_low.yaml's handoff stage.
The verdict that got walked back

The D1 N=50 × 3-seed validation was written up as PROMOTE, then corrected the same day to HOLD when the comparison was found to be against a favourable reference rather than a same-specimen-set control. It was subsequently re-run against a genuinely independent ALL_ANTS_CLEAN holdout. The overclaim is in the commit history on purpose — the verdict was not rewritten after the fact.

Adoption of scale_cap rests on two independent arguments that currently point the same way: non-inferiority on genus, and mesh-validity on its own original target (anterior joint-scale pathology). Any future recipe change must re-check pathology separately — a genus-neutral change could still reintroduce the explosion scale_cap exists to prevent.

custom_processing/prepare_antscan_data_for_mesh_fitting_alphawrap.py

The alpha-wrap reconstruction pipeline: what generalises, and what was heuristic all along

Observation

The CGAL alpha-wrap tiering was tuned on the 50-ant bench corpus. The question was whether it generalises to arbitrary insect meshes — so Phase-1 instrumentation (geometry-aware tier routing, per-specimen bisection timeout, near-touch normal diagnostic, p5 thin-feature diagnostic, hard compute ceiling) went in and ran across all 50.

Result
  • gap_ratio is a genuine predictor of difficulty (ρ = −0.537, p = 0.0001) that explains only ~37% of variance. Adding hole count, boundary edges, surface area, compactness, hole density and near-touch normal angle pushed R² to at most 0.41. Treat it as a routing signal, not a predictor of required alpha.
  • The ×5 "preserved-start" multiplier is not calibrated. Actual alpha/pre_gap across 45 successes spans 0.625–26.25 — a >40× spread. Tier A is clean (35/35, zero fallback); Tier B/C boundaries are not (2 of 3 fallbacks were mis-routed).
  • All 6 fidelity-gate failures fail via the same check — whole-surface p99.9 deviation, 3.1–4.3× over threshold — and never via gap fusion. Consistent with either a too-tight threshold or a real defect no other metric covers.
  • A reproduced MemoryError: Blender's bundled Python lacks embreex, so the p5 metric's ray-casting blows up on large meshes. 2/50 real crashes.

Deliberately stopped here. The remaining ~60% of difficulty variance is unexplained by any simple global geometric quantity tested, and the next step is visual ground-truth labelling, not more heuristic search. An inspection set is built and waiting.

Building the instrument

Aug 19 – Aug 20
feature/investigation
Built

diagnostics/correspondence_accuracy/

A metric that answers the correspondence question directly

Observation

Everything measured so far — D1 promotion, the GNC arms, stratified sampling — was scored by chamfer, edge and F-score. Those can improve while which scan point got matched to which anatomical region stays broken or gets worse. Without a direct instrument, "the constraint helped" cannot be told apart from "the loss landscape got smoother."

Design

For each sampled scan point on a synth_clean specimen with known ground truth: the true anatomical label (exact, because the corpus is a posed copy of the template — face topology asserted equal every run) versus the matched label (which region the fitted mesh's nearest vertex belongs to).

The matching direction is deliberately the fitter's own rule — the same knn_points nearest-vertex assignment TargetPartition.update uses. The metric audits the fitter, not a reimplementation of it. Output is full confusion matrices at three granularities: leg-level (6×6 cross-leg swaps), within-leg segment-level (restricted to already-correct legs, isolating femur↔tibia from leg swaps), and antenna — tracked separately because antenna is never its own TargetPartition group in any run, so its correspondence is presently unconstrained by construction.

This is the instrument every result in threads 6–9 is scored on: leg_acc, seg_acc, and the confusion structure underneath them. Its bootstrap noise floor at n = 12 is wide — a fact that later invalidated several small-sample figures, correctly.

anatomical_pose_init

CHAIN_OF_THOUGHT_20260820_21.md → SESSION_SYNTHESIS_20260825.md

Can a better leg-pose initialisation escape the basin problem? No — and the reason is structural

Premise

Thread 1 established that pose is trapped, not under-optimised. If the optimiser cannot leave a bad basin, start it in a good one. A prior hand-designed initialiser (cheap_anatomical_init.py) had already failed — worse than zero-init on almost every metric — so this thread tried a learned regressor instead.

A bug first

Before any comparison was valid: the 5000-specimen training corpus was generated with SMIL_OmniAnt.pkl (10229 verts, 13 PCs); the benchmark and the real fitter use OmniAnt_25PCs_joint_limited.pkl (10235 verts, 25 PCs). The trained checkpoint was topologically invalid for the benchmark it was to be compared against. Root cause traced through config.py: SMAL_FILE/N_BETAS resolve from an env var at import time, so a --model CLI flag can never switch the model. Fixed by making the generator assert and fail loudly.

Result A/B/C
ArmInit errorleg_accall_feature_RChamferF@0.01

C wins every metric at near-identical scalar pose error to A. So scalar error does not explain the outcome — which raised the real question, and produced the strongest single result of the thread.

The transferable mechanism

At matched ~23–30° magnitude, where the error sits dominates how big it is. Proximal error (coxa/trochanter/femur) → leg_acc 0.622–0.768. Distal error → 0.937–0.940. And coherent error (one clean rotation at the coxa, downstream joints at rest) → 0.839 against incoherent proximal noise at 0.622. A donor specimen's fully-valid pose (swap) is nearly as damaging as bad noise — so this is a basin-selection problem, not an anatomical-plausibility one. This says something about SMILify's optimiser in general, independent of whether any initialiser ships.

Then: real scans

On bench50, the learned init lost to zero-init (chamfer 0.000697 vs 0.000637, 20/50 wins, p = 0.013). The domain-gap hypothesis — synthetic training pose-noise too large versus near-rest museum specimens — was tested by three independent retrain-and-refit cycles at falling pose_scale: 23° → p=0.013 worse; 13.8° → p=0.85 parity; 8.85° → p=0.855, chamfer tied, 26/50. Monotonic and controlled. The broader lesson: a synthetic distribution's noise statistics, not just its coverage, must be calibrated to real deployment data — or a learned corrective network is worse than doing nothing.

Then: the closure

Three independently estimated coherent coxa-only candidates were built (PCA, cluster-axis, tip-direction) on the theory that coherent-beats-proximal made this an estimation problem. An oracle check on clean, noise-free points settled it before any GPU was spent: all three estimators agree with the true overall coxa→tip direction to within 4–5°, and still imply a coxa rotation ~50–55° from the true one — because the true coxa rotation's own implied direction differs from the true overall direction by a mean of 50.03°. Real leg poses distribute about half their bend across tr/fe/ti/ta.

No coxa-only estimator, however accurate, can recover the true per-joint decomposition. All three arms duly lost to zero-init (0.826–0.835 vs 0.867–0.872).

And the last door

If hand-built estimators can't capture the coupling, learn it: a PCA subspace over real whole-chain rotations, leave-one-out, IK constrained to that subspace. Every mode count tested (2–15) performed at or worse than raw IK's own oracle, with nearly flat explained-variance ratios (0.14, 0.12, 0.10…) — no detectable low-dimensional structure in 66 pooled rows.

The decisive step was checking for a bigger corpus before concluding anything: joint_limits.py's bounds are hand-authored in Blender by an artist, not calibrated from digitised poses; AntScan holds pinned museum scans with no per-joint rotation ground truth at all — extracting it would require solving the very correspondence problem this work exists to fix. The 12 synth_clean specimens are the only source of ground-truth joint rotations anywhere in the project.

Both families closed for the same underlying reason: geometry structurally underdetermines pose, and there is no data to learn a substitute from. That is what forced the reframe. (Two things do ship regardless: RigNet-style nearest-bone-segment leg assignment, macro-F1 0.656 → 0.831, and the same fix for body-core, recall 0.366 → 0.663.)

PHASE10_DESIGN → LAB_RECORD_correspondence_20260827.md

Stop regressing pose. Predict, for each scan point, which template vertex it is.

Theory

Every closed door in thread 6 was closed by trying to recover pose from geometry that underdetermines it. The registration-SOTA literature (GeoTransformer, D3Feat, REGTR) points at a better-posed target: correspondence first, pose derived from it. A dense oracle handed the fitter true per-vertex correspondence and moved seg_acc 0.832 → 0.874, 12/12 — the target was known to be worth chasing; what was unknown was whether a network could supply it.

C1 · Ask first

Before spending GPU on a head, measure whether the frozen backbone already carries usable structure. Within-specimen retrieval looked excellent (0.92–0.97 of ceiling) — and was measuring the wrong thing, since pose-dependent features ace it while being useless for correspondence. Cross-specimen is the real number: 0.27–0.55, far above chance on 4 of 5 segments, and the error splits by direction — strong along the limb (0.14–0.57), near chance around it (0.09–0.20). The weakness is specifically circumferential. That measurement, not preference, chose the head design: SurfEmb says a deterministic embedding cannot fix this and a contrastive one can.

C2 · Price the conversion

Two queued fits turned network output into pose via a centroid/IK conversion. Rather than read them as "does the network help", they were used to price the conversion itself — network-fed vs. true labels through the same conversion vs. the dense oracle.

Routeseg_acc
Dense oracle (direct vertex identity)0.8745
True labels → centroid/IK conversion0.8522
Network → centroid/IK conversion0.8488

The conversion discards ~41% of the achievable gain and flattens the gap between perfect and predicted signal to 0.003. The conversion, not the network, was the bottleneck. It also explained an old puzzle: the trochanter was the worst segment under conversion and the best under direct retrieval.

C3 · The head

Replace only the decode head and its loss: a continuous per-point embedding, one learned embedding per template vertex, correspondence by nearest neighbour, both sides L2-normalised so the training loss optimises exactly the retrieval performed at inference. Supervised by true vertex index, InfoNCE.

Cross-specimen retrieval improved on 4/5 segments, all p < 6e-3. The circumferential component — the reason for the design choice — improved 2–4×, from 0.09–0.20 to 0.28–0.43. The literature-derived prediction was made before the head was written and confirmed by the specific thing it predicted.

C4 · Into the fitter

Aggregate predictions into "where does the network think vertex v sits on this scan", add a masked term pulling fitted vertex v there. Additive, flag-gated, off by default.

seg_acc 0.8657 against a 0.8201 baseline — 84% of the dense oracle's gain from predictions alone, and better than the oracle fed true labels through the conversion. Worse information through the right mechanism beats perfect information through a biased one.

C5–C7 · Deployability
  • Which points to trust, without ground truth. Four candidate confidence signals tested. Cycle consistency cut mean retrieval error 0.726 → 0.449; similarity raised it to 1.044 — similarity is entangled with ambiguity, since a confusable point scores high precisely because it is confusable. MNN and multi-sample stability did nothing. Cycle filtering made the pipeline ground-truth-free end to end.
  • A written-down prediction that was wrong. All-segments was pre-registered to lose to the tr/fe/co whitelist. It reached 92% of the oracle gain instead — and it needs no whitelist, so it is the deployable configuration.
  • n=48 power run. At 12 specimens the configurations were indistinguishable. On 48 fresh specimens: +0.031 seg_acc, 37/48, significant on all three tests — real, robust, and smaller than the 12-specimen estimate of +0.050. The corrected figure is the one carried.
  • A three-way shuffle control, and what it revealed. Real correspondence on tibia/tarsus (0.8701) is indistinguishable from shuffled-within-segment (0.8711), sign p = 1.0 — while removing them entirely costs ~0.02. The head is doing two different jobs: genuine correspondence on tr/fe, and a coarse regional pull on ti/ta where vertex identity is irrelevant. This closed a planned class-balanced retrain before it was built.
C8–C10 · Real ants

Normalised into the model frame, the retrieval distribution on real scans stays healthy — coverage, entropy and per-segment shares track synthetic closely. No collapse. But cycle consistency, the signal the filter depends on, degrades 2.6× and unevenly — which makes it double as a per-specimen triage signal. Cause investigated across all 50: not scan damage, not point density, not species (within-genus spread exceeds between-genus); partly aspect ratio (ρ = −0.502, ~25% of variance, in the direction opposite a simple distribution-distance story).

C11–C12 · The one that didn't take

Circumferential hard-negative mining failed its pre-registered circumferential bar while genuinely improving direct retrieval — and C12 then showed tibia's role in the fitter did not change at all. Third case in the session of a retrieval gain that never reaches the fitter.

A hypothesis tested before it was funded

The obvious explanation for weak circumferential retrieval was geometric: a leg is a cylinder, a cylinder's cross-section has no unique axis, so the signal is architecturally unrepresentable — which would justify an equivariant backbone. Measured instead: real trochanter/femur neighbourhoods score 0.38–0.44 against a perfect cylinder's 0.031 floor, 12–14× above it (metric verified on toy shapes with known answers, which reversed the first reading). Ant leg segments are tapered and flattened, not cylinders. The signal is present and unlearned — a training problem, not an architecture problem, and far cheaper.

The coxa resisted four separate mechanisms — and the cause was a dropped term

Observation

With correspondence working corpus-wide, the residual leg-level error localised: it is coxal. Everything else had moved; the coxa had not.

The cost

  • Not controllable by the shape prior. Adding w_beta_prior = 0.002 made it worse (+60.8%) — the Mahalanobis prior pulls betas toward typical under the model, not correct for this specimen, and on a corpus with sampled shape parameters those directions diverge.

Also settled: it was never a capacity problem

A perfect parameter set scores 0.0908 on the coxal metric — the metric's own floor, because adjacent coxae are close enough that ~9% of coxal points misassign even on an exact mesh. Model expressiveness was never the limitation.

REGISTER_F_series_20260828.md — bars fixed before every run

Seven cheap arms to find out which advanced method, if any, is worth funding

F1

Is an equivariant retrain justified? A linear probe on the frozen backbone recovers circumferential angle at 52.9° against a 90° chance level — on the exact tensor the embedding head consumes — with a passing axial control (R² 0.85) and a passing shuffle control (89°). Meanwhile retrieval top-1 is 0.071.

The information reaches the head; the contrastive loss is not using it. A loss problem, not a backbone problem. Do not spend the GPU budget.

F2

Multi-hypothesis selection. A trained 1-of-5 selector combining similarity margin, cycle-consistency and local agreement closed 2.5% / 4.2% of the oracle gap against a 40% bar. Worse than cycle-consistency alone. The lesson is specific and non-obvious: rank accuracy is not a proxy for error reduction — the classifier picked the best candidate ~50% more often than chance and bought almost nothing, because the cases it fixed and broke roughly cancelled.

F4

Optimal transport instead of per-point argmax (Sinkhorn, SuperGlue-standard). Real, extremely consistent, and just under the bar: tr −4.3% (35/40), fe −4.5% (33/40), both p = 0.0000, against a registered ≥5%. Recorded FAIL; the bar had no PARTIAL band and one was not invented after seeing the number. Mechanism checks pass (entropy 4.31 nats, 1.19× the vertices argmax uses — spreading, not collapsing).

A first implementation used balanced OT and its registered no-op control fired at 0.096. The arm was recorded VOID rather than quietly patched, and rebuilt as unbalanced OT where τ→0 provably recovers argmax — control then passes at 1.000.

With F2, two independent attacks on the selection stage both land in the low single digits. Selection is not where the headroom is.

F5

Conformal uncertainty — SMILify has no way to know a fit is wrong on a real scan. Coverage landed inside the band (0.871) but that half is guaranteed by construction; the width condition failed at 1.078 against a bar of <0.80 — the signal-based intervals are wider than a constant predictor's.

The registered mechanism check is worse than the endpoint: per-segment coverage collapses on co (0.657), and worst-decile coverage is 0.200. The intervals cover 87% of specimens and 20% of the specimens a user actually needs flagged.

Conformal machinery worked exactly as advertised and produced honest, useless intervals. The missing ingredient is a signal that predicts error — and neither cycle-consistency nor embedding margin is one at segment level. (This bounds the earlier triage claim's scope; it does not overturn it — that was a per-specimen binary signal, this is a per-segment quantitative one.)

F6

Geodesic-softened targets. The loss is cross-entropy against one true vertex, so on a 10,235-vertex mesh the vertex 0.1 mm away is punished as hard as one on the opposite leg. Targets spread over the true vertex's graph neighbourhood — graph, not Euclidean, because adjacent coxae are physically close but not edge-connected, and Euclidean smoothing would teach exactly the cross-leg confusion. Verified before launch: σ read off the mesh, cross-leg target mass masked to exactly 0.0 (it was 0.46 on one coxa).

Median 3D error 0.02687 → 0.02606, −3.0%, against a registered −15%. Mechanism check passes. One run, no replicate, within epoch-to-epoch fluctuation. Essentially nothing.

F7

Is 16 dimensions too few for 10,235 vertices? A free diagnostic on the frozen key table.

Now standing discipline

Every arm carries a mechanism check alongside its endpoint, and every bar is written down before the run. The D1 arm cleared its endpoint by 127% and was still wrong; the probe in (4) produced a believable pattern from random weights. Endpoints alone are not evidence — and load_state_dict(..., strict=True) plus one named-tensor comparison against the raw file is now mandatory before anything downstream is trusted.

Two smaller pieces of hygiene from the same weeks: jobs are submitted under --account=rwth2151, never default (which is exhausted and silently demotes priority); and .gitignore patterns like probe_*.py were found to be the root cause of a recurring vanishing-scripts problem — two real modules were recovered from it.

Blocker, found while writing the spec

The endpoint needs per-specimen deform_verts, and the A/B outputs in this repo do not contain them — only analysis.json and a figure. The raw fits went to JSC scratch. integrity_anterior_full_corpus.json stores deform_mag_mean globally, not stratified by anatomical group, even though SCHEMA.md's own secondary endpoint specified stratification. Routes, in order: recover the scratch fits (zero GPU), or refit the 81 ALL_ANTS_CLEAN specimens on both arms (two short jobs, reported as n = 81 and never as the A/B result). The 126 local fits are scattered smoke tests with no matched pair — checked, not usable.

Either way it pays
  • PASS (head ratio rises ≥ 0.10, sign p < 0.05) → representation bounds are the mechanism behind a whole-animal failure, demonstrated on something already in production. The global-prior path gets its first working instance instead of a literature argument.
  • FAIL (tail down 58%, symptom unmoved) → the seventh instance of this session's central pattern, and all three of §6.10's named mechanisms are then exhausted. The anterior becomes genuinely open and needs a learned global prior — the expensive path, which this cheap arm exists to justify before anyone starts it.

The registered mechanism check guards the failure mode that matters: if the head ratio and the antenna ratio rise together, that is a global deformation-scale shift and is reported as such regardless of the primary endpoint. A void control requires arm A to reproduce §6.10's 0.72× within ±0.03 before any verdict is issued at all.

The one decision that is yours, not another experiment's

The coxa fix costs +16.3% shape-parameter accuracy. It is not controllable by the shape prior, and it is uncorrelated with the coxa gain — so it is a real, characterised trade, not a bug waiting to be found.

Is a 16.3% shape-accuracy cost acceptable in exchange for a 2.3× closer coxa and leg_acc 0.9418 → 0.9682? That depends on how SMILify's downstream users actually consume shape parameters. No further experiment on this side resolves it.

Ranked, deliberately not started

  • R1 — re-score the shipped scale bound against the §6.10 symptom (thread 10). Cheapest, decisive either way, and it tests the family the record itself identifies as the only one that has ever moved correspondence. Registered; blocked only on recovering the converged fits.
  • A global deformation / plausibility prior. The strongest remaining path, and the family w_offset and w_midline already belong to. One constraint to scope first: it cannot be learned from the pipeline's own registrations — a leave-one-out shape model over them reconstructs a held-out specimen no better than the population mean (gen/spread 0.99–1.00), while the same metric reaches 0.42 at 10 modes and 0.00 at 20 on synthetic data with exact correspondence. The synthetic route is open; the real-registration route is closed.
  • Human joint annotation on real scans. Tooling drafted, 10 specimens scoped and sanity-checked — one per genus across 8 subfamilies, chosen for body-plan extremes that stress the fitter differently (trap-jaw Odontomachus, flattened Cephalotes, spined Acromyrmex, long-legged Cataglyphis, tiny cryptic Discothyrea). This is the only thing that can turn thread 7 from "the fit gets geometrically closer" into a correspondence claim. Note it yields joint positions — a pose-accuracy measure, not leg_acc/seg_acc, which are surface-label metrics and need a different definition built from annotations.
  • Mean-teacher fine-tuning on bench50. The last untried lever with genuine upside on real data, fully pre-registered. It should follow the decision above, not precede it.
  • Alpha-wrap visual ground truth (thread 4) — inspection set built, waiting on labels.
The honest limit, stated first rather than buried

bench50 has no correspondence ground truth. Everything in thread 7 is geometric fit — "did the fit get closer to the scan" — never "is the correspondence correct." Closing that gap is what the annotation work is for, and it does not exist yet.

Every result above is committed with its pre-registration timestamped before its run. Where a bar was met by the letter but the instrument was later found wrong, both are recorded — no verdict was rewritten after the fact.
