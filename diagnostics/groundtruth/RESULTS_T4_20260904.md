# T4 — measuring the mesh surface recovers ~2.8× more morphological signal than measuring the rig

Same 757 production fits, same criterion, same 27 genera (386 specimens). The only thing that
differs is **what is measured**: distances between rig rotation pivots, or distances between
anatomical landmarks on the fitted surface.

Bar fixed before running: surface must beat joints by **1.5×** in mean F-ratio (between-genus to
within-genus variance) with the genus-bootstrap CI of the difference excluding zero.

## Result

| feature set | n features | mean F |
|---|---:|---:|
| joint-derived (rig bone lengths, Mosimann log-shape-ratios) | 49 | **1.82** |
| surface-derived (GLAD trait ratios) | 5 | **5.10** |

**Ratio 2.81×.** Paired genus-bootstrap difference **+3.21, 95% CI [+1.71, +5.20]**. Bar cleared.

Not a mean effect concealing overlap — **every surface trait beats every joint feature**:

| surface | F | | best joint | F |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| HW/WL | **8.63** | | seg_b_a_2 | 3.31 |
| HW/HL | 5.46 | | seg_b_a_4 | 2.91 |
| PetL/WL | 4.82 | | seg_b_a_3 | 2.84 |
| GL/WL | 3.60 | | seg_l_3_ta_r | 2.81 |
| HL/WL | 3.00 | | seg_l_3_ta_l | 2.64 |

## Four confound checks, all passed

**1. Feature count is not the explanation.** Under genus-label permutation both sets null at
**1.01** (joint, CI [0.88, 1.13]) and **1.00** (surface, CI [0.73, 1.28]). Comparing 5 features
against 49 does not bias the statistic; the observed 1.82 and 5.10 sit far above a common null.

**2. Fit quality explains part of it, not most.** Partialling out log registration error per feature
drops the ratio **2.81× → 2.28×**. So roughly a fifth of the advantage is fit-quality structure
correlated with genus; the remainder is not, and 2.28× still clears the bar.

**3. The implausible tail was suppressing it, not inflating it.** Dropping the 9.1% of fits with
biologically impossible traits *raises* the ratio to **2.94×** (368 of 386 retained).

**4. No single genus drives it.** Leave-one-genus-out over all 27: ratio ranges **2.46× to 2.93×**,
median 2.82×. The minimum comes from dropping *Cephalotes*, the morphological extreme most likely to
be doing the work — and even then the advantage is 2.46×.

## What it means

**The measurement class matters, independently of fit quality.** A rig joint is a rotation pivot
with no anatomical definition and no surface to be measured against; an anatomical landmark on the
fitted surface is a defined point. On identical fits, the second carries roughly three times the
genus signal.

This reframes G13. Joint-derived proportions score R ≈ 0 against expert ground truth, and the
template-regression story (G1: the fit is 17% better than the population mean; WL varying only 8.7%
CV across 757 morphologically extreme ants) explains part of that — but **not all of it is the
fitter. Part of it is that rig bone lengths are the wrong thing to measure.**

**Concrete recommendation for SMILify: report surface-derived traits, not rig-derived ones.**
`diagnostics/groundtruth/trait_extract.py` implements this.

## Limits

- **Genus discriminability is not accuracy.** This shows surface traits separate genera better; it
  does not show the values are *correct*. Only expert trait landmarks can establish that, and this
  result is the argument for obtaining them, not a substitute.
- **5 surface features.** They are every ratio the current landmark set supports, so they were not
  selected on outcome — but a 5-feature mean is less stable than a 49-feature one. The bootstrap
  resamples genera, not features.
- **The surface landmarks are not anatomically adjudicated.** Five of ten are marked
  `source="verify"` in `landmark_template_indices.json`. The advantage is measured with provisional
  landmarks and could move once they are corrected — in either direction.

## The ask this licenses

Joint-derived measurements score **R ≈ 0** against expert-corrected truth (G13, n = 12). Surface-
derived traits carry **2.8× more signal** on the same fits. Five landmarks — **HL, HW, WL, ML, SL** —
decide whether that signal is real accuracy. That is the highest-value outstanding request in the
project, and it is roughly twenty minutes of expert time.
