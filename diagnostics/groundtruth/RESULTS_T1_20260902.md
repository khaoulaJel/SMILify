# T1 — 9.1% of production fits emit biologically impossible traits, and bilateral asymmetry cannot see them

> **CORRECTED 2026-09-02, after T2.** The original version of this file claimed that registration
> error *anti*-predicts trait validity (Spearman −0.356) and therefore that *neither* cheap QC
> signal detects these failures. **That claim was an artefact of the statistic and is withdrawn.**
> See "Correction" below. The asymmetry result and the 9.1% count are unaffected and stand.

First measurement on the new GLAD trait layer (`trait_extract.py`), over all **757** Z8 production
fits. No ground truth, no annotation, no new compute.

## The headline

**69 of 757 fits (9.1%) produce at least one biologically impossible trait**, against bounds set
before looking at any outcome and set deliberately wide — values no ant has, not tuned thresholds.

| ratio | bounds | median | min | max | flagged |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ML/HL | [0.10, 2.00] | 0.945 | 0.116 | **4.817** | 44 (5.8%) |
| HW/HL | [0.40, 2.00] | 1.096 | 0.398 | **5.132** | 33 (4.4%) |
| SL/HL | [0.20, 3.00] | 1.046 | 0.209 | 4.034 | 7 (0.9%) |
| GL/WL | [0.50, 4.00] | 1.614 | 0.095 | 3.486 | 2 (0.3%) |
| PetL/WL | [0.02, 0.80] | 0.212 | 0.030 | 1.715 | 1 (0.1%) |

Because the bounds are wide, **9.1% is a lower bound on the broken fraction.**

**The failures are one-directional.** All 44 ML/HL violations are *too large* (none too small); 32 of
33 HW/HL violations are too large. The failure mode is **inflation**, not collapse.

## The part that matters: both cheap QC signals miss this

The two quality signals available without an annotator are the optimiser's own residual and
bilateral self-consistency. Neither detects these failures.

**Bilateral asymmetry is blind to them — and inverted.** Specimens flagged by ML/HL have
**4.7%** median mandible asymmetry against **11.0%** for unflagged ones. The impossible fits are
*more than twice as bilaterally consistent* as the rest.

That is not noise, it is structure: **both mandibles are inflated equally.** Asymmetry is a noise
detector and is constitutionally blind to symmetric, systematic error — Insight IV's bias/noise
distinction, observed directly rather than argued.

**Registration error DOES predict ML validity — positively.** Spearman(ML/HL, registration error)
is **+0.465**; larger mandible ratios go with worse fits. Local mandible residual tracks it more
strongly still (**+0.651**, T2). The impossible mandibles sit on genuinely bad fits.

**Consequence: chamfer is a usable QC signal for this failure class; bilateral self-consistency is
not, and is actively misleading.** That is a narrower claim than the original version of this file
made, and it is the one the evidence supports.

## The mechanism hypothesis was tested and REFUTED (T2)

The original version proposed that the area-sampled data term rewards inflating thin structures.
T2 pre-registered that as two predictions plus a control, and **both failed**:

- **P2 failed with the opposite sign.** Spearman(ML/HL, mandible-region residual) = **+0.651**
  (p = 1.6e-92). Inflation makes the local fit *worse*, not better. The objective is not rewarding it.
- **The control failed.** The mesosoma — the thickest, best-sampled part — shows the same
  "distortion goes with lower local residual" pattern (−0.217, p = 1.6e-09) that the hypothesis
  predicted would be absent. The effect, whatever it is, is not thin-structure-specific.

P1 did pass and stands on its own: the mandible carries **4.86%** of the objective (legs 47.4%,
gaster 20.6%, head 10.0%, mesosoma 8.8%, antenna 8.5%), a concrete number for the sampling-imbalance
claim. But the mechanism is withdrawn.

## Correction: why the original −0.356 was wrong

The withdrawn claim used Spearman between registration error and **|log deviation of ML/HL from the
corpus median|**. That number reproduces exactly (−0.356 stored, −0.370 recomputed), so it is not a
data error — it is the wrong statistic for this failure mode.

**|log deviation| is two-sided; the failure mode is one-sided.** All 44 ML/HL violations are *too
large*; none are too small. ML/HL correlates **+0.465** with fit error across the corpus, so
specimens far *below* the median have both a large |log deviation| and a *good* fit. Splitting the
corpus confirms it: below the median, error vs ML/HL is **+0.317**; above it, −0.098. The
below-median half drives the negative sign.

Using a symmetric deviation statistic on an asymmetric failure mode inverted the correlation.
**The lesson is the statistic, not the data**: score directional failure directionally.

## Context: the trait layer is responsive (validity check, same session)

Before this, two pre-specified taxonomic predictions were confirmed on the same trait table, so the
instrument demonstrably moves with the animal:

- **Cephalic index** HW/HL: *Cephalotes* 1.522 > *Pheidole* 1.146 > *Odontomachus* 0.793.
  Predicted ordering, **1.92× separation, Cohen's d = 2.19**.
- **Scape index** SL/HL: *Eciton* 1.533 highest, *Cephalotes* 0.978 lowest — both as predicted.

Neither prediction derives from the pipeline, so this is not circular.

## Limits, stated plainly

- **Plausibility bounds are mine, not adjudicated.** They are deliberately wide and one-sided in
  effect, but they are literature-informed judgement, not a published standard. Fabian should
  confirm them; a tighter, expert-set bound would raise the flagged fraction.
- **Impossible ≠ the only broken.** A fit can be wrong while staying inside every bound. This
  measures the catastrophic tail only.
- **The −0.356 inversion is one correlation on one trait**, and ML depends on the highest-risk
  landmark in the set (the mandibular apex, protocol risk R1, on a template that is a *mean*
  mandible). It could be a landmark artefact rather than a fitter artefact. Distinguishing them
  needs the expert-adjudicated apex.
- Registration error is a chamfer residual; it is the optimiser's own objective, so "the objective
  does not predict validity" is the honest reading, not "the fit is bad".
