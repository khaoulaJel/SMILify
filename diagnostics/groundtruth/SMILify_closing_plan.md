# Closing the loop: making SMILify a morphometrics instrument

**Companion to the annotation-validation brief.** Written to be handed to Fabian as the argument for what
the internship contributes to the SMILify paper, and as a work plan for the remaining time.

---

## 0. The one-sentence claim

> **Antscan turned ant collections into a scan archive. Nothing yet turns a scan archive into a
> measurement archive. A registered parametric model does — and this work establishes what it measures
> correctly and what it does not.**

Everything below is in service of making that sentence defensible.

---

## 1. What changed in the field while we were working (and why it decides the framing)

**Antscan** (Katzke, Hita Garcia, Lösel, … Economo, van de Kamp; *Nature Methods* 23:663–672, March 2026;
https://www.antscan.info) released **2,193 whole-body synchrotron micro-CT datasets covering 212 genera
and 792 species**, CC BY 4.0, including **ready-made 3D surface meshes (STL) for every specimen**,
generated by a Biomedisa neural-network segmentation.

Four properties of that release matter to us specifically:

1. **The meshes are exactly our problem class.** They are neural-network segmentations post-processed
   with largest-island selection. The authors state plainly that legs and antennae of larger specimens
   were prone to fall outside the field of view, that some specimens show soft-tissue shrinkage and
   decay, and that some datasets are partially truncated. Holes, noise, missing appendages, arbitrary
   global pose, segmentation artefacts — the exact list in the internship description, now attached to a
   2,000-specimen public corpus.

2. **Scale is recoverable.** Scans used three magnifications with effective pixel sizes of **1.22 µm,
   2.44 µm and 6.11 µm**, recorded per specimen in the metadata. This resolves item **A4** of the brief:
   for Antscan the size axis is *not* lost. Proportion-only analysis is a property of our current scAnt
   corpus, not a property of the method.

3. **The authors name our gap as the open problem.** Their discussion states that obtaining quantitative
   information from micro-CT scans remains challenging and that new automated-analysis bioinformatics is
   needed to unlock databases like Antscan. A parametric-registration measurement layer is a direct
   answer to a problem posed by the dataset's own authors.

4. **The precedent for payoff exists.** Matte et al. (*Sci. Adv.* 11, eadx8068, 2025) analysed cuticle
   versus body volume for 507 Antscan species and found thinner cuticle associated with larger colonies
   and higher diversification rates. That is one scalar per specimen producing a macroevolutionary
   result. A validated trait vector per specimen is a much larger surface.

**Consequence for the paper:** the deliverable is not "we can fit meshes." It is "here is the measurement
layer for scan archives, and here is the honest reliability statement that comes with it." Antscan is the
destination corpus even if we never fit all of it during the internship — fitting **20–50 Antscan
specimens** spanning subfamilies is enough to make the claim concrete and is achievable now.

## 2. Why *parametric registration* rather than "just measure the mesh"

This needs one paragraph in the paper, because a reviewer will ask it.

Segmentation gives you a surface. It does **not** give you correspondence. You cannot ask a raw STL where
the posterior basal angle of the metapleuron is. Registering a common template gives, for every specimen:

- **Dense correspondence** — every vertex means the same thing across all specimens. Any measurement
  becomes a deterministic function of the fitted mesh, computed identically for every specimen, with zero
  inter-observer variance.
- **Retrospective measurability.** New trait defined next year? Recompute over the whole fitted archive in
  minutes; no return to specimens.
- **Homologous landmarks for free**, which is the input format geometric morphometrics actually wants.
- **Pose–shape separation.** Museum specimens are in arbitrary, non-canonical poses. Linear measurements
  taken on a raw mesh are pose-contaminated; a skinned model factors pose out. This is a real and
  underappreciated advantage over 2D image-based morphometrics.
- **A rigged, animatable model as a by-product** — the replicAnt/WOLO link (§5).

That list is the argument. Frame the contribution as *correspondence*, with measurements as the
demonstration.

## 3. Speak the biologists' language: measure GLAD traits, not rig joints

This is the single highest-leverage change to the annotation protocol, and it dissolves Insight VI
(convention mismatch) rather than working around it.

Ant functional ecology has a **standard, published, diagrammed trait set** — the GlobalAnts / GLAD schema
(Parr et al., *Insect Conserv. Divers.* 10:5–20, 2017; trait definitions at
https://globalants.org/static/trait-descriptions.pdf; measurement conventions on AntWiki,
https://www.antwiki.org/wiki/Morphological_Measurements). The core traits:

| Trait | Definition (abbreviated) | Ecological reading |
|---|---|---|
| **WL** Weber's length | Diagonal of mesosoma in profile, pronotum/cervical-shield junction → posterior basal angle of metapleuron | The standard body-size proxy in ant ecology |
| **HL** head length | Anterior clypeal margin → posterior cephalic margin, full-face view | Body size; mandible musculature |
| **HW** head width | Maximum head width, dorsal view, across the eyes | Body size; bite force; used habitat complexity |
| **HW2 / eye position** | Width behind eyes; EP = HW2 − HW1 | Visual ecology, foraging strategy |
| **ML** mandible length | Mandibular apex → anterior clypeal margin | Prey size, diet |
| **SL** scape length | Length of antennal scape | Chemosensory reach, foraging |
| **EL** eye length | Maximum eye diameter | Diurnality, predation mode |
| **FL** hind femur length | Femur length, hind leg | Running speed, size–grain / habitat structure |
| **PW** pronotum width | Maximum pronotum width, dorsal view | Body size, gap traversal |
| **PetL** petiole length | — | Trophic position (strong predictor in Neotropical ants) |
| **TBL** total body length | Sum of mandible + head + WL + petiole (+ postpetiole) + gaster | Size |

Three things follow.

**(a) Annotate trait endpoints, not rig joints.** These definitions are protocol-fixed, published with
diagrams, and unambiguous to a myrmecologist. A rig joint is a rotation pivot with no anatomical
definition; validating against it produces apparent error that is definition mismatch. Keep the rig-joint
annotations as a separate, secondary table (they are still useful for pose accuracy) and make the traits
the headline.

**(b) Traits are conventionally size-corrected by WL.** Every study cited above divides each trait by
Weber's length before analysis. That is *exactly* the proportion-only regime our fitting operates in.
Our current limitation is the field's standard practice. Say so; it removes an apparent weakness.

**(c) Traits map onto ecological questions.** Head width and mandible length carry diet and bite-force
signal; femur length carries locomotion and habitat-structure signal; eye metrics carry foraging-mode
signal. That is the "why would anyone care" paragraph, and it is not speculative — those relationships
are established in the literature and currently limited by measurement throughput.

### Proposed landmark schema (~30 points, covers all traits above)

Record every point with a **type label**, because error is type-dependent and reviewers in morphometrics
expect this (Bookstein typology):

- **Type I** — anatomically discrete junctions (antennal insertion, pronotum/cervical-shield junction,
  posterior basal angle of metapleuron, mandibular apex, anterior clypeal margin midpoint).
- **Type II** — curvature extrema (posterior cephalic margin midpoint, petiolar node apex).
- **Type III** — constructed extremal points (maximum head width L/R, maximum pronotum width L/R, eye
  anterior/posterior margin). These are *sliding* along one axis by construction and will have larger,
  direction-dependent error. Report them separately; do not average them in with Type I.

Points: head (clypeal margin midpoint, posterior cephalic margin midpoint, max head width L/R, head width
behind eyes L/R, eye anterior/posterior L/R, mandibular apex L/R, antennal insertion L/R, scape apex L/R);
mesosoma (WL anterior, WL posterior, max pronotum width L/R); waist (petiole anterior/posterior,
postpetiole where present); gaster (anterior margin, apex); legs (hind femur proximal/distal L/R, hind
tibia distal L/R). Store bilateral pairs explicitly — left–right disagreement is a free noise estimate (§4).

## 4. The validation design: two tiers of ground truth

Ten specimens cannot support a reliability certificate. They can support a *protocol* — and the second
tier below gives external validation at taxonomic scale with **no additional annotation**.

### Tier 1 — Per-specimen human ground truth (tight, small n)

- **Choose the specimens for spread, not convenience.** Maximise coverage of the shape space: different
  subfamilies and body plans (e.g. a *Camponotus*-type formicine, a *Pheidole*-type myrmicine, a ponerine,
  a *Strumigenys*-type miniature, a *Cephalotes*-type aberrant, a leaf-cutter). With n = 10, spread is
  worth far more than replication.
- **Blind re-annotation of all ten** (brief item A1) — this is not optional. Without it every error figure
  is an upper bound confounded with your own uncertainty. The mouse-skull registration-landmarking
  literature does exactly this: intra-observer error is subtracted from automated–manual distance to give
  a conservative registration error, and landmarks whose error exceeds the observer floor are flagged as
  problematic (Percival et al. 2019; Devine et al., registration + deep learning for automated landmark
  detection). Adopt that reporting convention directly — it is the accepted standard in the exact method
  class we are in.
- **Report per landmark and per trait:** mean signed error (bias), mean absolute error (precision),
  annotation repeatability (the floor), and the ratio. A trait is "supported" only if bias is small
  relative to between-species variance *and* error exceeds the annotation floor by a defensible margin.

### Tier 2 — Population-level external validation against GlobalAnts (free, large n)

For species present in **both** Antscan and the GlobalAnts trait database, compare automated trait values
against published human measurements. These are different individuals of the same species measured by
different people under the standard protocol — so this tests exactly the thing comparative biology needs:
**do species rank correctly on size-corrected traits?**

This is not a substitute for Tier 1 (different specimens, so per-specimen error is unrecoverable), and it
is not circular (independent measurers, independent specimens, independent institution). It is the
cheapest large-n accuracy check available, and no one has run it for an automated 3D pipeline.

### The tests that decide the paper's claims

1. **Bias decomposition (brief item B3).** Regress log(fitted / true) on log(size) and on the leading
   shape PCs. Constant multiplicative bias → correctable post hoc, comparative claims survive.
   Shape-dependent bias → not correctable, comparative claims fail. State the threshold before running it.
2. **Precision ≠ accuracy, made concrete.** Refit the same mesh N times from perturbed initialisations
   (PointNet init vs. perturbed init vs. random). The spread is precision. Compare against Tier-1 accuracy.
   This is Insight I as a figure.
3. **Bilateral asymmetry as a free noise floor.** Ants are near-symmetric. Measured left–right disagreement
   in fitted traits is a lower bound on pipeline noise requiring **no ground truth at all** — runnable on
   all 2,193 Antscan meshes. Cheap, scalable, and a genuinely nice trick for the paper.
4. **Degradation curves (assignment 5).** Ablate clean meshes systematically — hole fraction, vertex
   noise σ, decimation ratio, appendage truncation, pose perturbation — and plot trait error against each.
   Output: a table telling a collection manager *what scan quality is needed for which trait*. That table
   is the most immediately useful thing in the paper for a practitioner.
5. **Leave-one-clade-out generalisation.** Retrain the shape space excluding a subfamily, fit its members,
   report Chamfer + trait error. This is SMAL's Fig. 11 argument (generalisation to unseen species) and it
   needs no annotation. It also honestly bounds the model's morphological envelope.
6. **Procrustes-contamination audit** on any classification/regression computed from aligned shape data,
   per arXiv:2601.18448.

## 5. The WOLO / replicAnt link — now partly built

`SMILyANT` (a PCA-driven parametric ant model) is already a built-in subject model in replicAnt as of the
March 2026 Unreal Engine 5.4 migration. The generator half of the argument is therefore not hypothetical.

The dependency direction stated in the brief holds and should be stated explicitly in the paper: fitted
shapes seed synthetic corpora; systematic bias in fitting becomes invisible, internally-consistent bias in
every network trained downstream. **Validation is upstream of the WOLO extension.**

And with Antscan voxel spacing restoring absolute scale, the joint architecture sharpens: **shape from
registration, absolute size from metadata (Antscan) or from learned appearance (WOLO, for field images
with no reference).** Published log–log allometries between linear ant traits and dry mass then convert a
validated WL or HW into a biomass estimate — the quantity ecologists actually use.

## 6. What to write, and in what order

**Title direction:** *A parametric shape model for ants, and what it can and cannot measure.*

Two contributions, in this order:

1. **The first parametric statistical shape model for a hyperdiverse insect clade**, fitted robustly to
   scan-derived meshes with real defects, with an explicit statement of its morphological envelope.
2. **A validation protocol for automated morphometrics** that separates precision from accuracy and bias
   from noise, with the worked demonstration that in-distribution synthetic calibration certifies
   measurements that fail against human truth.

**Figure list (aim for these; each maps to a section above):**

- F1. Pipeline: raw Antscan/scAnt mesh → cleaning → PointNet init → part-aware → non-rigid SMIL fit.
- F2. Fits across the morphological range, including the failures. Show the failures.
- F3. Trait definitions overlaid on the fitted mesh next to the GLAD diagram — the "we measure what you
  measure" figure.
- F4. Per-trait accuracy vs. annotation floor (Tier 1), Type I / II / III separated.
- F5. GlobalAnts cross-validation scatter, size-corrected (Tier 2).
- F6. Bias decomposition: constant vs. shape-dependent.
- F7. Degradation curves — the practitioner's table.
- F8. Leave-one-clade-out generalisation.
- F9. Old synthetic-calibration reliability table beside the human-truth one. This is the methodological
  punchline and should not be buried.

**Negative results are load-bearing.** A pipeline that says which of its outputs are trustworthy is more
useful than one claiming uniform confidence, and the failure modes are properties of the method class, not
of our implementation.

## 8. Standing rules carried forward

- Pre-register the bar and the voiding checks before each run; voiding checks fail closed on missing data.
- Score proxy and mechanism separately.
- Re-measure every anchor on the current pipeline before expressing anything as a distance from it.
- Never validate a model against data that model generated.
- Report accuracy, not lift; use the majority-class rate as baseline under imbalance.
- Report a reliability figure beside every measurement and conspecific agreement beside every
  classification claim.
