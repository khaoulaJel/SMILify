# R5 — no annotation-free proxy detects the named-part misplacement. Corpus prevalence cannot be
# estimated from the fits alone, and that is itself a finding about the pipeline.

Job 3858106, 20 s, all 757 fits. Three proxies computable from a fitted mesh alone, validated
against R4's per-specimen named-part error on the 12 annotated specimens (range 5.4–63.4%).

| proxy | what it measures | Spearman ρ | p | usable? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **bilateral** | centroid of part `X_r` vs the mirror of `X_l` — a symmetric animal should have these coincide | +0.47 | 0.124 | no |
| **buried** | fraction of the anterior parts' vertices lying deep inside the mesh rather than near its outer shell | +0.24 | 0.452 | no |
| **extent** | each anterior part's spatial extent relative to the same part on the template | −0.52 | 0.080 | no |

Bar was ρ > 0.5 at p < 0.05. **None passes.**

## What this means

**Corpus-scale prevalence cannot be estimated without more annotation.** The 12 annotated specimens
carry the mechanism; they cannot carry a claim about how often it happens across 757.

**But the failure is informative, and it strengthens the R3/R4 result rather than weakening it.**
The misplacement is invisible to every self-consistency signal available from a fit:

- It is **not** bilaterally detectable — the parts are misplaced *symmetrically*, so left and right
  agree with each other while both are wrong. This is the same structure T1 found for the impossible
  traits, where flagged specimens were *more* bilaterally consistent than unflagged ones.
- It is **not** a burial or collapse artefact — the parts are not squashed inside the mesh; they sit
  on plausible surface, just not the correct surface.
- It is **not** a gross extent anomaly — the parts are roughly the right size.

> **A fit with its mandible 50% of a body length out of place looks, by every internal measure, like
> a good fit.** Chamfer is low, the surface is close, the parts are symmetric and correctly sized.
> Nothing short of external anatomical ground truth reveals it.

That is the sharpest available statement of why this gap went unnoticed, and why surface-based QC
cannot be relied upon for anatomical claims.

## Consequence for the paper and the pipeline

The honest scope is: **characterised on 12 specimens; corpus prevalence not established.** That is a
normal limitation rather than a hole, and R5 documents that it is not fixable by cleverness — only
by annotation.

The cheapest thing that would establish prevalence is the 15 stratified specimens already selected
in `v4_annotation_targets.json`, since R4 shows the effect is largest on unusual morphologies
(*Eciton* 63.4% versus *Leptogenys* 5.4%) and that set was chosen to span exactly that range.

## Limits

- Three proxies, chosen for being cheap and interpretable, not exhaustive. A learned detector trained
  on the 12 might do better, but validating it would need the same annotations it is meant to avoid.
- n = 12 for the validation, so a real correlation below ~0.7 would likely be missed. `extent` at
  ρ = −0.52, p = 0.080 is the closest to significant and is worth revisiting if the annotated set
  grows.
