## The optimiser did not fail — the problem is degenerate

Median reflection residual **0.96%** of body scale; median plane stability under 50% subsampling
**1.1°**. The fit converged tightly and reproducibly. **It converged on a symmetry the body genuinely
has, which is not the sagittal one.**

The recovered normals sit at a **median 86° to the body's long axis** — i.e. the planes *contain*
the long axis, which is exactly what a sagittal plane does. **But so does every other plane through
that axis**, and an ant's head–mesosoma–gaster mass is close to a solid of revolution about it. All
such planes are near-equally symmetric, so reflective symmetry of the body **cannot single out the
sagittal one**. The optimiser lands on whichever is marginally best, essentially at random in roll.

> **The features that would break the degeneracy — legs, antennae, mandibles — are precisely the
> ones that had to be masked out, because their posture is what defeated R7a–c in the first place.**

That is a genuine catch-22, and it is structural rather than a matter of thresholds or weighting.

## Consequence: the R7 branch closes

Per the stopping rule fixed before the run, **no further midline variants, SDF thresholds,
centroid constructions, bilateral-pair combinations or symmetry heuristics will be tried.**

Four independent constructions have now failed, and the diagnosis is consistent across all of them:

| attempt | midline from | why it failed |
|---|---|---|
| R7a | fitted body centroid | offset; both head sides on one side |
| R7b | fitted bilateral part pairs | 1/12 valid — parts misplaced (R2/R4) |
| R7c | expert bilateral joint pairs | 2/11 valid — pair axes disagree 12–27° from splayed legs |
| **R8** | **body reflective symmetry** | **4/12 — the body is rotationally degenerate about its long axis** |

**SCOPE OF THE CLAIM — stated narrowly on purpose.** This is *not* "geometry cannot determine
anatomy". It is:

> **For these imperfect, pinned ant scans, the geometric information available to the four tested
> annotation-free procedures does not provide a stable enough bilateral reference to recover
> laterality reliably.**

Four procedures, one specimen set, one scan modality. A different acquisition (unpinned, symmetric
posture, complete meshes) or a procedure using information none of these used could succeed. What is
established is that the *cheap, annotation-free* routes available here are exhausted — which is the
justification for moving to a learned method, rather than a claim about geometry in general.

R8 is the strongest evidence in the set precisely because **the optimiser did not fail.** It found a
highly symmetric plane, consistently, across subsamples. It simply was not the uniquely meaningful
one. An optimisation failure would have left the question open; a converged fit on a degenerate
objective closes it.

## What this does and does not close

**Closed:** the geometric route to automatic laterality. R6 showed SDF finds appendage material but
merges left and right; R7/R8 show the merge cannot be undone with an annotation-free reference frame
derived from the scan or the fit.

**Not closed, and unaffected:** R3's finding that naming the part is worth 34.7% → 0.2%; the
capacity result; the two-channel result; the measurement convention. **The value of the part label
is established — only this particular way of obtaining it is now excluded.**

**What remains** is learned semantic/anatomical correspondence, where laterality is inferred from
appearance and context rather than from a geometric frame. That is where the literature already sits
(`LITERATURE_VALIDATION_20260908.md`), and R6–R8 have now removed the cheap alternative that would
otherwise have had to be tried first.

## Limits

- One plane parameterisation, three PCA initialisations, Nelder–Mead. A global search could find a
  better optimum, but the residual and stability numbers say the current optimum is already tight —
  the issue is that many optima are near-equal, not that the search is poor.
- The body mask comes from one SDF threshold. A different mask changes which near-symmetric plane
  wins; it does not remove the degeneracy.
