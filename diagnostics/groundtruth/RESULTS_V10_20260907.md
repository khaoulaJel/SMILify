# V10 — V8's *percentage* was split-dependent, but the *endpoint* is not: supervision drives
# held-out landmark error to ~7% whichever landmarks are held out. That looks like a floor.

Job 3838981, 3m53s. Same loss, same λ=1, same 12 specimens as V8 — only **which four landmarks are
held out** changes.

**PRE-REGISTERED BAR: FAILED.** The bar was that every split must improve by >20%. One does not.

| split | held out (production) | held out (supervised) | change | chamfer | joints |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S1 left+rear *(V8's split)* | 12.9% | **7.4%** | **42%** | 1.07× | 32.8% |
| S2 right+front | 10.9% | **8.0%** | 26% | 1.13× | 33.1% |
| **S3 head midline** | **7.2%** | **7.0%** | **3%** | 1.12× | 33.2% |
| S4 mixed | 10.4% | **6.7%** | 36% | 1.12× | 32.9% |

Improvement ranges **3% to 42%**, median 31%. **V8's headline 47% was measured on the split with
the worst production baseline**, and is not representative.

## The pattern the bar could not see — post-hoc, and flagged as such

The *starting* points vary a lot (7.2% to 12.9%). The *ending* points barely vary at all:
**7.4, 8.0, 7.0, 6.7 — a spread of 1.3 points across four disjoint landmark subsets.**

S3 does not fail because supervision stopped working there. It "fails" because the head-midline
landmarks were **already at 7.2% in the production fit** — there was no room to move. Every split
converges to the same place; only the distance travelled differs.

**This is a post-hoc observation, not the pre-registered test, and is weaker evidence than if it
had been predicted.** But it changes what the number means: the honest statement is not
*"supervision improves held-out landmarks by 47%"* but

> **supervision drives held-out surface-landmark error to ≈7% of Weber's length regardless of which
> landmarks are supervised, from wherever production happened to start.**

## Why ~7% matters

Three independent lines now point at the same value:

- V8: held-out error **6.9%** at λ=1
- V9: arm B **7.6%**, arm D **7.1%**
- V10: **7.4 / 8.0 / 7.0 / 6.7%** across four disjoint splits

A quantity that lands on ~7% no matter which subset is optimised, on a term that drives its
*supervised* points to 0.1%, is behaving like a **floor**, not like a converging optimisation.

**The candidate explanation is human placement variability**, and it is still unmeasured. If a
second annotation of the same specimen disagrees with the first by ~7% of Weber's length, then
every result above has reached the instrument's ceiling and further tuning is measuring noise.
If it disagrees by ~1–2%, the floor is something else — correspondence, or the shape space — and
there is real headroom.

**This makes the repeat annotation the single highest-leverage outstanding action in the project.**
It is ~10 minutes, and it determines whether V8/V9's numbers are a result or an artefact.

## Also replicated

- **Chamfer cost is stable at 1.07–1.13×** across all four splits. The near-free surface cost of
  landmark supervision is not split-dependent.
- **Joint error is unchanged in every split** (32.8–33.2%), replicating V8 and V9's finding that
  surface → joints is absent, now four times over.

## What this does to arm B's asymmetry

V9 flagged that arm B's bilateral asymmetry rose (0.044 → 0.101) and suggested it might be an
artefact of holding out the left-side landmarks. V10 does not test asymmetry directly, so this
remains open — but S2 (holding out the *right* side) reaching the same endpoint as S1 argues that
nothing about the left/right choice is special.

## Limits

- n = 12, one run per split, no seed replication; the 1.3-point spread across endpoints is within
  what this fitter's run-to-run variation could produce.
- The four splits are not independent — they draw from the same 12 landmarks on the same 12
  specimens, so a landmark held out in one split is supervised in another.
- The floor interpretation is post-hoc. It is a hypothesis generated by V10, to be tested by the
  repeat annotation, not a conclusion V10 establishes.
