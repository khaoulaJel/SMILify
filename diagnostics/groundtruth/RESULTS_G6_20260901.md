# G6 — Case B stands, and the reason is sharper than "the objective is wrong"

Joints fitted to the human annotations under a hard projection back into the model's own shape
prior (`|z| <= k` after every step), sweeping k, with chamfer reported at every level so a solution
that buys joints by wrecking the surface is visible rather than hidden.

| setting | betas \|z\| | joint residual | chamfer | total objective |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **production fit** | **0.81** | **38.86%** | **0.00094** | 0.00086 |
| oracle, \|z\| ≤ 1 | 0.99 | **11.01%** | 0.03206 | 0.03636 |
| oracle, \|z\| ≤ 2 | 1.94 | 10.69% | 0.03076 | 0.03736 |
| oracle, \|z\| ≤ 3 | 2.85 | 10.34% | 0.02998 | 0.03752 |
| oracle, unconstrained | 26.65 | 6.45% | **1.06853** | 0.71693 |

## The headline

**At |z| ≤ 1 — the same shape plausibility the production fit already uses (0.81) — a solution
exists with 11.01% joint residual against production's 38.86%.** Three and a half times more
anatomically accurate, inside the prior, no exotic parameters.

**Case B stands.** The representation is not the binding constraint, and this time the oracle is
plausible: |z| = 0.99, essentially identical to production's own shape magnitude.

The unconstrained row is retained only to show what G3 and G4 were actually measuring: |z| = 26.65
and chamfer **1.069**, three orders of magnitude worse than production's 0.00094 in a space
normalised to extent ~1. That solution was never near-truth in any useful sense.

Note also that plausibility is nearly free: going from |z| ≤ 1 to |z| ≤ 3 buys only 11.01% → 10.34%.
**The accuracy is available at the first standard deviation.**

## But "the objective is wrong" is not the right diagnosis

The plausible oracle is 3.5× better anatomically and **34× worse on chamfer** (0.032 vs 0.00094).
The production objective therefore genuinely prefers the production solution — it is not misreading
its own terms. So the earlier B1 phrasing is too crude, and the real finding is structural:

> **Nothing in the objective constrains where the skeleton is.** Chamfer, edge, normal, laplacian,
> offset, symmetry, midline, limit and scale are all surface or regularisation terms. `limit`
> bounds joint *rotations*, not joint *positions*. So the skeleton is free to be anatomically wrong
> provided the surface matches — and the surface can be matched by many different internal
> skeletons.

That is the shrink-wrapping pathology the reports have flagged repeatedly from the metric side
("every per-part metric in this suite is source→target proximity, and shrink-wrapping games it"),
now measured from the parameter side against human truth.

**The objective is not mis-weighted. It is under-determined with respect to the skeleton**, and no
reweighting of existing terms fixes an unconstrained quantity. This is why every reweighting
experiment in the Z-series returned null.

## What this licenses, and it is concrete

**Add a term that constrains joint position.** That is a new term, not a new weight — which is
exactly the class of intervention the project has never tried, and it explains why the ones it did
try could not work.

The obvious objection is that annotations are not available at inference time. The answer is that
they do not need to be: a *predicted* skeleton is enough, and predicting 3D joint positions from a
mesh or from images is a solved-enough problem in this ecosystem — it is what OmniTrax does for
pose, and the 14 annotated specimens are the seed of a supervised set for a 3D joint predictor.
That closes the loop with the WOLO/replicAnt line rather than opening a new front.

## The experiment that should come next

This measured the two corners of a trade-off; it has not shown whether the middle is empty.

> **Pareto sweep.** Fit with `chamfer + λ · joint_target`, sweeping λ over several decades, and plot
> joint residual against chamfer. If a knee exists where chamfer is near production's 0.00094 and
> the joint residual is near 11%, the two objectives are compatible and the fix is simply the
> missing term. If the frontier is steep everywhere, they are in genuine conflict and the surface
> data cannot identify the skeleton — which would be a much deeper statement about what
> registration from surface geometry alone can achieve.

That single curve decides whether the fix is cheap or fundamental, and it needs no new annotation.

## Provenance, stated because two earlier attempts were wrong

G3's 3.7% and G4's "B1" verdicts were both artifacts and are withdrawn: G4's first run mapped
annotations with the wrong frame (Blender transforms on `.obj` import; targets were crushed to
~0.02 extent, caught because production read 4722% against G1's known ~28%), and both G3 and G4's
second run used an oracle unconstrained in `betas`, which reached the joints by leaving the shape
space. Only G6's plausibility-bounded rows are load-bearing.

The production row's 38.86% here is higher than G1's 28.2% because the frame is solved once against
the production joints rather than re-solved per evaluation; it is the same quantity measured under
a slightly stricter alignment, and all rows in this table share it, so the comparison is internally
consistent.
