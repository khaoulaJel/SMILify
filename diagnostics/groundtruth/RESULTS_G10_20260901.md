## The result

**The joint term rescues the measurements.** At a realistic σ = 15% it takes median trait R from
**−0.059 to +0.171** — from *no signal whatsoever* to a real, if modest, correlation with truth.
That is the first intervention in this entire investigation to move a morphometric endpoint.

**But it does not beat reading the predicted skeleton directly.** At σ = 15%, T = +0.336 against
J = +0.171. J − P = +0.230, just under the +0.25 bar; J − T = **−0.164**, the wrong sign.

**Verdict: PARTIAL**, exactly as the pre-registration defined it — measurements improve, but no
better than skipping the fit.

## The crossover, which is the actionable finding

T degrades much faster than J as targets worsen:

| σ | T | J |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 0.506 | 0.461 |
| 15% | 0.336 | 0.171 |
| **20%** | **0.123** | **0.153** |

**J overtakes T at roughly σ = 20%.** The fit is flatter under noise — the surface term and shape
prior stabilise it — while raw targets inherit their error directly. So the recommendation is
conditional and quantitative rather than binary:

> **Below ~20% predictor error, measure the predicted skeleton directly. Above it, fit.**

That is a usable engineering rule, and it needs a predictor's error to be *measured*, not assumed —
which is now a specification rather than a guess.

## Why fitting loses at low noise, and it is not a bug

The σ = 0 row is diagnostic: with **exact** targets the fit still reaches only R = **0.507**, while
the targets are 1.000 by construction. **The fit does not fully honour the skeleton it is given.**

The likely mechanism is **shrinkage toward the mean shape.** `betas` are clamped to |z| ≤ 1 and the
surface term pulls every specimen toward configurations the shape space prefers. That *reduces
absolute joint position error* — which is exactly what G9 measured and reported as denoising — while
*compressing between-specimen variation*, which is precisely what `R` measures. A method can be
simultaneously more accurate per specimen and worse at ranking specimens, and that appears to be
what is happening.

**This reconciles G9 and G10, which otherwise look contradictory**: G9's endpoint is absolute
position, G10's is relative ranking, and a shrinkage estimator improves the first at the expense of
the second.

**It is a hypothesis, not a measurement.** The direct test is to compare the spread (standard
deviation of log-shape-ratios) of GT, targets, production and J: if J's spread is compressed
relative to GT, shrinkage is confirmed and the fix is to relax the shape prior when the joint term
is active. That is one short run and it is the obvious next step.

## What this does to the programme

**G7–G9 remain valid but are now correctly scoped.** They establish that the objective is missing a
joint-position term, that adding it is nearly free in surface fit, that it needs near-complete
coverage, and that it improves *joint positions* even from noisy targets. G10 shows that
improvement reaches the *measurements* — but that for the measurement task specifically, the fitting
step is not adding value below ~20% predictor error.

**That is not an argument against SMILify.** The fit still produces the mesh, the correspondence and
the shape space, none of which a skeleton predictor provides, and G10's endpoint is bone-length
proportions only — the traits that happen to be joint-derived. It is an argument against using the
fitted skeleton as the source of *those particular measurements* when a good predictor exists.

## Limits

n = 14; independent Gaussian target noise (the easy case, per G9's caveat); λ fixed at 0.1;
`R` at n = 14 has a wide interval so the block-level values are indicative and the median across
blocks is the number to read; and only joint-derived traits can be validated at all, because
ground-truth part dimensions would need annotated surface landmarks, which do not exist yet.
