# G9 — the fit denoises its own targets. A mediocre predictor is enough.

Full coverage, λ = 0.1, isotropic Gaussian perturbation of every joint target, σ in % of each
specimen's mesosoma length. Target error is **measured**, not assumed. Job 3448280, 4 min.

| σ | measured target error | **fit vs truth** | fit / targets | vs production | chamfer |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 0.00% | **9.26%** | — | 0.24× | 0.00216 |
| 5% | 7.32% | 10.95% | 1.50 | 0.28× | 0.00216 |
| 10% | 15.39% | **14.90%** | **0.97** | 0.38× | 0.00227 |
| 15% | 22.47% | **19.20%** | **0.85** | 0.49× | 0.00226 |
| 20% | 31.44% | **23.52%** | **0.75** | 0.61× | 0.00246 |
| 30% | 44.48% | **31.47%** | **0.71** | 0.81× | 0.00293 |

**Control passes:** σ = 0 gives 9.26%, reproducing G7's ~9.0% at λ = 0.1, so this is the same
experiment with one factor added.

## The result: DENOISING

**From 10% target error upward the fit lands closer to truth than the skeleton it was handed**, and
increasingly so as the targets get worse — ratio 0.97 → 0.85 → 0.75 → 0.71. The surface objective
is correcting the predictor rather than merely obeying it.

**And the term never hurts.** At every noise level tested, including targets that are 44.5% wrong,
the fit still beats the production baseline of 38.9%. There is no tested regime where adding the
joint term is worse than not adding it.

The one ratio above 1.0 (σ = 5%, ratio 1.50) is not a failure and should not be read as one: the
fit's own floor is 9.26%, so it cannot beat a 7.32% target. **A method cannot denoise below its own
accuracy limit.** That is a ceiling on the benefit, not a defect in the mechanism.

## The mechanism, and why it makes sense

The fit behaves as a shrinkage estimator: it has an intrinsic accuracy floor near 9% and pulls
noisy targets toward it. Where the targets are better than the floor, the floor dominates; where
they are worse, the surface term and the shape prior recover most of the difference. That is
exactly the behaviour a well-posed regulariser should show, and it is the opposite of the
pass-through outcome that would have closed the deployment path.

## The specification this produces

Read off the table, for whoever builds the predictor:

| predictor median joint error | resulting fit | improvement over production |
|---:|---:|---:|
| ~7% | 11.0% | 72% better |
| ~15% | 14.9% | 62% better |
| ~22% | 19.2% | 51% better |
| ~31% | 23.5% | 39% better |
| ~44% | 31.5% | 19% better |

**A predictor only has to beat ~44% median joint error to help at all, and one at 15% already
recovers most of the available gain.** Combined with G8, the full requirement is:

> **near-complete coverage of the skeleton, at modest accuracy.**

Coverage is the hard constraint; accuracy is not. That is a favourable specification, because dense
prediction with mediocre per-joint accuracy is a much easier target than sparse-but-exact.

## The caveat that matters most

**Isotropic independent Gaussian noise is the easiest error structure to denoise.** A real predictor
errs *systematically* — correlated across joints, concentrated on the hard ones (distal tarsi,
pretarsi, antennae, exactly where G1 showed the pipeline already fails), and biased rather than
zero-mean. Independent noise partly averages out across 49 joints; correlated bias does not.

So these magnitudes are informative and the direction is trustworthy, but **the denoising ratio is
optimistic**, and the honest next step before committing engineering effort is to repeat this with
structured error: correlated across joints, concentrated distally, with a non-zero mean. That is a
small change to the noise model in this same script.

Also: n = 14, λ fixed at 0.1 rather than swept jointly with σ, and the joint term uses a plain
squared distance — a robust kernel would likely denoise better still.

## Where this leaves the programme

G7 showed the objective is missing a joint-position term and that adding it is nearly free in
surface fit. G8 showed the term does not propagate, so the skeleton must be nearly fully covered.
G9 shows the accuracy required of that skeleton is modest, and that the fit improves on what it is
given.

**Together those three results are a complete feasibility statement for the fix**, and the
remaining risk is concentrated in one place: whether the denoising survives realistic, structured
predictor error.
