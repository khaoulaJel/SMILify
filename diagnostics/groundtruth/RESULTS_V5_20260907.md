## The decision, answered — but read the margin

The pre-set rule was: best joint error at ≤1.5× production chamfer must beat production by 20%.
Result: **26.17% vs a bar of 26.19%.** It passes by **0.02 percentage points**. Treated as a clean
pass that would be overstating it — the honest reading is that anatomical accuracy is improvable at
modest surface cost, and the cheap end of the curve is a *marginal* rather than decisive win.

**The strong version of the finding is further up the curve, and it is a trade, not a free lunch:**
λ=0.03 halves joint error (32.7% → 16.6%) for 1.57× chamfer; λ=0.1 cuts it 76% for 2.30×. Every
unit of anatomy costs surface fit roughly proportionally. **This is NOT the Pareto improvement the
roadmap set as the target** (surface 1.03× / joint 0.55×). What we have is surface 1.16× for joint
0.80×, or surface 2.30× for joint 0.24×. A real, large, controllable effect on a real endpoint —
but bought, not free.

## Three things that are new

**1. The anterior responds, and responds harder.** It starts far worse than the posterior
(36.97% vs 24.38%) and closes most of the gap by λ=0.1 (11.65% vs 7.41%). Supervision reaches
exactly the region V2/V3 localised the pathology to.

**2. The flagged specimen benefits most.** Eciton — the only trait-flagged specimen in the set —
goes 84.58% → 44.21% at λ=0.03 → 21.77% at λ=0.1. Its production fit is catastrophically bad
(2.6× the corpus median) and supervision rescues it fastest. **And this is before it had any
anterior annotations at all** — this run used the 35-joint file, body and legs only.

**3. THE IMPORTANT ONE: joint accuracy and trait validity move in OPPOSITE directions.**
Violations climb monotonically with λ (1 → 2 → 5 → 6 → 8) precisely while joint error collapses.
Fits that are anatomically *more correct against human annotations* produce *more* biologically
impossible surface traits.

That directly opposes [[V4]], where the hinge drove violations 9.1% → 0.1% while touching nothing
else. **The two interventions pull against each other**, and it means neither endpoint alone can
adjudicate: the hinge wins on violations by construction, the joint term wins on annotation error
by construction, and each degrades the other's metric.

It also corroborates V1's mechanism from a second direction: the joint term buys joint accuracy
partly by distorting the surface, which is what breaks the traits.

## What this licenses, and what it does not

**Licensed:** joint supervision is a real, large, controllable improvement on *real annotation
error*, measured against corrected ground truth, with a passing control. Task 2's decision — "if
none of these can improve anatomical accuracy without destroying surface fit, rethink the
direction" — comes back **do not rethink; continue**.

**Not licensed:** any claim of a free improvement, and any claim about surface landmarks. The
`+landmarks` and `+both` arms were **not runnable — no surface-landmark annotations exist for any
specimen.** `04_blender_trait_annotator.py` is written and ready but has never been run. Half the
2×2 is still missing, and it is the half the roadmap argues is decisive.

**The obvious next test is the combination**, because V4 and V5 fix each other's failure: joint
supervision for anatomy, hinge for trait validity. Neither V4 nor V5 alone tests that.

## Limits

- n = 12, one run per λ, no seed replication.
- Joint error is measured on the SAME joints used as targets — reachability, not generalisation
  (the G7 caveat still applies; G8 showed it does not propagate to unsupervised joints).
- Eciton contributed body and legs only. Its corrected 44-joint file is now installed
  (commit 49d6ea98) and a re-run would put the one flagged specimen into the anterior column.
- Violations here are counted over 12 specimens, not the 757-fit corpus.
