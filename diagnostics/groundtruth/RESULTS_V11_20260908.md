# V11 — human landmark repeatability is 2.4% of Weber's length. The ~7% floor is NOT annotation
# noise, so the remaining anatomical error is real and there is headroom.

Three specimens re-annotated independently, without reference to the first round:
`Formica_cf.fusca` (large, easy), `Gigantiops_destructor` (mid-size, large eyes),
`Discothyrea_patrizii` (smallest and worst-fitting). 12 landmarks each, 36 paired placements.

## The number

| specimen | median disagreement |
|---|---:|
| Discothyrea_patrizii | **2.1%** |
| Gigantiops_destructor | **2.2%** |
| Formica_cf.fusca | **2.6%** |
| **ALL 36 placements** | **2.4%** |

**Human landmark repeatability = 2.4% of Weber's length**, and it does not depend on specimen size:
the smallest specimen (WL 107 scan units) is as repeatable as the largest (WL 2007).

## The cross-check that settles it

V10 found held-out surface error floors at ~7% whichever landmarks are held out. If that floor were
annotation noise, splits made of hard-to-place landmarks should floor higher than splits made of
easy ones.

| V10 split | human repeatability | V10 endpoint | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| S1 left+rear | 3.5% | 7.4% | 2.1× |
| **S2 right+front** | **2.6%** | **8.0%** | **3.1×** |
| S3 head midline | 2.3% | 7.0% | 3.1× |
| S4 mixed | 2.0% | 6.7% | 3.3× |

**Every endpoint is 2–3× its own split's human noise.** S2 is built entirely from the most
repeatable landmarks — `mandibular_apex_r` (0.9%), `antennal_insertion_r` (2.4%), `scape_apex_r`
(2.7%), `wl_anterior_r` (5.0%) — and still floors at **8.0%**, the *highest* of the four.

> **The ~7% floor is not the annotation instrument. It is the model.**

Between 65% and 70% of the residual anatomical error is something the fitter or its correspondence
is doing, not something the annotator failed to see. **Real headroom remains**, and V8/V9's numbers
are a result rather than an artefact.

*(Spearman between split repeatability and split endpoint is +0.80, but on n = 4 with a range of
1.3 points that is not evidence of anything; the load-bearing observation is the 2–3× gap, not the
ordering.)*

## Two landmarks are much worse than the rest, and one is a tool problem

| landmark | median | note |
|---|---:|---|
| **antennal_insertion_l** | **37.5%** | see below |
| head_width_l | 11.1% | a maximum-extent judgement; genuinely harder |
| wl_posterior_r / wl_anterior_r | 5.1 / 5.0% | the two Weber points, as predicted |
| everything else | 0.9–2.8% | |

**`antennal_insertion_l` is not imprecision and not a left/right swap** — I tested the swap
hypothesis directly and round 2's left point is far from *both* of round 1's points on two of three
specimens. Meanwhile the RIGHT insertion is among the most repeatable landmarks in the set (2.4%).

A left/right asymmetry that large, on a bilaterally symmetric structure, points at the **viewing
setup**: the canonical pose presents the right side, and the left antennal socket is
poorly visible from it. **This is a defect in the annotation tool, not in the annotator.** Adding a
mirrored view for left-side landmarks would fix it, and until then `antennal_insertion_l` should be
treated as unreliable — including in V8's S1 held-out set, which contained it.

That the overall median is still 2.4% *despite* including a landmark at 37.5% is what makes the
headline robust.

## Limits

- n = 3 specimens, one repeat each — this estimates a median, not a distribution. Per-landmark
  values rest on 3 pairs each and should be read as indicative.
- Same annotator twice. This measures **intra-observer** repeatability, which is a *lower bound* on
  total annotation uncertainty; a second annotator would disagree more.
- Both rounds used the same tool and the same canonical view, so any bias baked into the view (as
  with `antennal_insertion_l`) is invisible to this test and does not appear as noise.

# V11b — the antennal-insertion outlier is BIMODAL, not noisy. My occlusion diagnosis was wrong.

A third round on the same three specimens, placing only `antennal_insertion_l` under a **pinned
definition** — *"where the antenna tube emerges from the head"* rather than *"the socket"*.

| specimen | r1–r2 | r1–r3 | r2–r3 | agreeing pair | outlier |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Discothyrea (smallest, worst fit) | 2.4% | **0.6%** | 2.3% | r1–r3 | r2, 2% away |
| Formica (largest, easiest) | 39.2% | 40.0% | **2.6%** | r2–r3 | **r1, 40% away** |
| Gigantiops | 37.5% | **3.1%** | 36.8% | r1–r3 | **r2, 38% away** |

**In every specimen two rounds agree to 0.6–3.1% and the third sits ~37–40% away — and which round
is the odd one out changes between specimens.** That is bimodal, not noisy.

**The landmark has two defensible locations about 37% of Weber's length apart**, and each round
picked one effectively at random. Within a location, precision is **2.6%** — indistinguishable from
every other landmark in the set.

## My occlusion hypothesis is refuted

I attributed the 37.5% to the left socket being hidden by the mandible and scape in full-face view.
**If that were the cause, the smallest and hardest-to-see specimen would be worst. It is the best**
— Discothyrea's three rounds agree to 0.6–2.4%, while the large, easy Formica is where a round went
astray. The annotator's own account is the correct one: the socket is a wide annular depression and
any point on its rim is defensible.

This is the same conceptual failure V7 found at the template level — *a landmark is not a point* —
reappearing inside the annotation protocol. The fix is the same in kind: **pin the definition to a
single geometric feature.** "Where the tube emerges" is one point; "the socket" is a ring.

The left-side view added earlier is retained — it makes the tube junction easier to see — but it is
no longer claimed as the cause.

## Effect on the noise floor

Substituting the within-location precision for this landmark leaves the overall figure essentially
unchanged: **2.4% → ~2.5%** (median over the 12 landmarks), with `head_width_l` (11.1%) now the
worst remaining. **The V11 conclusion is unaffected and slightly strengthened**: the model's ~7%
floor sits ~3× above annotation noise, and the headroom is real.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** only round 3 used the pinned rule, so **true pinned-rule repeatability
is not measured here** — that would need a fourth round. What is measured is the within-location
precision, which is what the substitution above assumes.
