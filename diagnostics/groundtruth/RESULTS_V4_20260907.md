# V4 — the hinge baseline: 9.1% → 0.1% for 1% more chamfer. And it proves less than it looks.

Job 3810117, 10m48s. 69 flagged + 120 sampled unflagged, re-optimised from the production
parameters with `L_production + w · hinge`. Pre-registered intent in
`ROADMAP_anatomical_supervision.md`: this is the **baseline the real method must beat**, not a
proposed solution.

**EQUIVALENCE CHECK PASSES.** The torch trait layer (including a differentiable Kabsch for head
width) matches `trait_extract` to **8.06e-07** relative, bar 1e-4. The hinge acted on the actual
GLAD ratios.

**VOIDING CONTROL PASSES.** `w=0` re-optimised from production leaves 63/69 still violating and
breaks 1/120 previously-clean specimens — a reconstructed corpus rate of **9.1%**, exactly T1's
number. The sweep measures `w`, not the optimiser.

## What this establishes — and what it does not

**Genuinely established, and these were the real questions:**
1. The violation removal is **nearly free in surface fit** — 1.01× chamfer, not the 2.3×–5.3× that
   the joint term cost in V1.
2. It **does not require a strong prior.** w=1 is enough; the effect saturates immediately.
3. It **does not break clean specimens.** 0/120 newly broken at every weight.

**NOT established, and this is the honest limit:**

**The violation count is not an independent endpoint here — the hinge optimises the very quantity
being scored.** Driving ML/HL below 2.00 necessarily removes an "ML/HL > 2.00" violation. That
9.1% → 0.1% is a report that the optimiser succeeded at what it was told to do, not evidence that
the anatomy improved. **A mandible pushed just inside the bound is not thereby a correct mandible.**
No annotation was used in V4 and no joint error was measured, so anatomical accuracy is untested.

**The 0.98× retention is weaker evidence than it appears.** The hinge is exactly zero inside the
bounds, so unflagged specimens receive **zero hinge gradient** by construction, and gradients do not
cross specimens in a batch. 0.98× therefore confirms the implementation has no leak — a real and
necessary check — but it is close to a tautology, not a discovery about biology.

**The compression question is still open, and it was asked about the wrong group.** What matters is
whether the **69 flagged** specimens retain their between-specimen differences or are all squashed
onto the boundary. Median severity goes 0.220 → 0.000, which is consistent with both "corrected"
and "flattened against the bound". V4 did not save the per-specimen flagged ratios, so this cannot
be computed from the stored results. **That is the one measurement missing before this baseline is
complete**, and it is a small patch plus an 11-minute rerun.

## The number to beat

> **9.1% → 0.1% impossible traits, at 1.01× chamfer, with unflagged variation retained at 0.98×.**

The supervised method must beat this **on anatomical accuracy**, not on violation count — because
on violation count the hinge is essentially unbeatable by construction, and that is precisely why
violation count cannot be the endpoint that decides between them.

## Limits

- 69 flagged + 120 unflagged, not the full 757; the corpus rate is an estimate, with the
  new-breakage term from a 120-specimen sample (0/120 bounds it under ~2.5% at 95%).
- 400 iterations from the production parameters, one run per weight, no seed replication.
- Trait **validity**, never accuracy. No surface ground truth exists.
