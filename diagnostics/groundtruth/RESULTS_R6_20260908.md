# R6 — the SDF part prior's instance recovery does NOT transfer to real scans. The blocker is
# laterality, exactly as the original probe predicted.

Job 3859489, 58 s, 12 annotated specimens, ~3.7 s each. Method is the original probe's own:
CGAL-style inward ray-cone SDF (trimesh + embree), the probe's global appendage threshold
(0.0307 × bbox diagonal), connected components of the thin side. Each expert appendage landmark is
assigned to its nearest component.

## Result

| | |
|---|---:|
| specimens where all four appendage landmarks land in **distinct** components | **2 / 12** |
| specimens where every landmark is within 5% of its component | 4 / 12 |
| median components on the thin side | **34** (template: ~9) |
| median time | 3.7 s |

**The dominant failure is left/right merging.** `mandibular_apex_r` and `mandibular_apex_l` land in
the *same* component on **9 of 12** specimens. The antennae merge on several more.

## This is the predicted failure, at a much larger scale

`sdf_prior_PROBE.py` reported on the template: *"5 of 6 leg instances recovered pure; the 6th
failure is a LATERALITY merge, not a body/appendage failure. That is the exact gap a
bilateral-symmetry prior fills."*

On the template that was **1 of 6**. On real ethanol scans it is **9 of 12** for the mandibles
alone. Real scans have 621–5521 connected components, are not watertight, and have touching or
fused limbs — so structures that are separate on a clean template are physically joined in the scan,
and no connectivity-based split can separate them.

**The two results in the original probe were on different data, and only the first transfers:**

| | measured on | transfers? |
|---|---|---|
| SDF **field** reproducible (r 0.77–0.94, <1.3 s) | real scans | **yes** — confirmed here, 3.7 s |
| part **instance** recovery (purity 0.98–1.00) | template only | **no** |

## What this means

**The automatic part label R3 needs is not available today.** The SDF field is sound, fast and
dependency-free, and it separates *body from appendage* — but turning that into *named, lateralised
instances* fails on real scans.

**And the blocker is specific and named:** laterality. Not segmentation quality, not the threshold,
not speed. Left and right structures are connected in the scan itself.

That is a much narrower problem than "build a part segmenter", and the original probe already
proposed the fix — a bilateral-symmetry prior. The model has an exact mirror map
(`trait_extract.mirror_map`, verified symmetric to 1e-3), and the fitted pose gives a midline. A
component straddling the midline is a merge and can be split geometrically rather than
semantically.

**Worth stating plainly: this was tested in 58 seconds rather than assumed.** Had the synthetic-data
segmenter route in `NEXT_STEP_SOTA_20260908.md` been started on the assumption that instance
recovery transfers, it would have hit the same laterality wall weeks later.

## Limits

- One threshold (the probe's template-derived 0.0307). A per-specimen or per-part threshold might
  reduce over-fragmentation, though it cannot fix a physical connection between left and right.
- Nearest-component assignment is crude; a landmark near a boundary could be attributed either way.
  The 9/12 mandible merges are not boundary cases — both landmarks fall in the *same* component.
- Only the four appendage landmarks were scored. Body parts, which the field separates well, were
  not the question.
