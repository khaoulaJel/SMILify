# G8 — the joint term does not propagate. Coverage, not just accuracy, is the constraint.

Bar and decision rule fixed in `PREREGISTRATION_G8_heldout.md` before the run. Job 3448002, 12 min.

## The voiding control passed, so the rest is readable

**Scheme D — 50% of joints supervised with targets permuted across joints** — gives a held-out
change of **−10.1%**, i.e. scrambled targets make the unsupervised joints *worse*. Real targets do
something anatomically meaningless ones do not, so the effect measured below is not "any extra term
stabilises an under-determined fit".

Note the scrambled arm's supervised residual sits at **54–56%**, against 5–6% for true targets: the
optimiser cannot satisfy incoherent targets, which is the expected behaviour and confirms the term
is doing what it claims.

## Primary endpoint — FAIL

**Scheme A, the deployable case** (supervise body axis + proximal leg joints, hold out distal and
the entire anterior):

| λ | supervised | held-out | production baseline | relative | specimens better | p |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.03 | 11.17% | 56.44% | 59.02% | **+4.4%** | 9/14 | 0.42 |
| 0.1 | 5.53% | 57.37% | 59.02% | +2.8% | — | — |

The bar was ≥20% relative with p < 0.05. **The term does not propagate: supervising the proximal
skeleton leaves the distal and anterior joints essentially where they were.**

## The substantive finding is the dose-response

| supervised | held-out relative improvement |
|---:|---:|
| 10% | −5.5%, −0.2% |
| 25% | +8.6%, +4.6% |
| 50% (random) | +6.8% … +13.3%, median **+8.9%** |
| 50% (anatomical: proximal → distal) | **+4.4%** |
| **75%** | **+23.7%, +32.3%** |

**Benefit to unsupervised joints scales steeply with how many joints are supervised, and only
becomes substantial above ~75%.** Supervising a tenth of the skeleton does nothing at all.

## The mechanism hypothesis is refuted, and that is the interesting part

The pre-registration proposed that the 25 betas, which couple every bone length simultaneously,
would let proximal supervision pull the shape toward truth so distal joints followed unsupervised.

**They do not.** Scheme A (+4.4%) is *worse* than random supervision of the **same number of joints**
(+8.9%), despite proximal joints being the larger, better-supported ones. If shape coupling were
carrying the signal, anatomically coherent proximal supervision should have been the best case, not
the worst.

The explanation is that a distal joint's position depends on distal bone lengths *and* distal
rotations, and `joint_rot` is free per joint. Whatever the shape space contributes proximally, the
distal rotations can absorb. **The kinematic chain does not transmit the constraint downward.**

## What this changes about G7's deployment path

G7 showed that supervising the *complete* skeleton cuts joint error 38.9% → 9.0%. G8 shows that
benefit does not extend to joints left unsupervised. Together:

- **A sparse predictor is not enough.** Predicting a handful of reliable landmarks and expecting the
  rest of the skeleton to follow will not work — that is exactly scheme A, and it returns 4.4%.
- **A predictor must cover essentially the whole skeleton.** That is a materially harder engineering
  problem than G7 alone suggested, though not an unreasonable one: dense 3D joint prediction is
  what pose-estimation networks do, and the coverage requirement is a specification rather than an
  obstacle.
- **G7's headroom is real but conditional.** It is reachable only with near-complete supervision,
  so it should be reported as "what the objective is missing" plus a stated coverage requirement,
  never as an achieved improvement.

## The question this leaves, and it is now the decisive one

G8 settles **coverage**. It says nothing about **accuracy**: every target here was a human
annotation, i.e. exact. A deployed predictor will be noisy.

> **Next: noise tolerance.** Perturb the joint targets with controlled noise (a sweep from 0 to well
> beyond expected predictor error) at full coverage, and measure where the benefit disappears. If
> the 38.9% → 9.0% gain survives targets that are themselves 10–15% off, a realistic predictor
> suffices and the path is open. If the benefit collapses once targets are as noisy as a predictor
> would be, then the joint term needs ground truth to work, and G7 is a diagnosis rather than a fix.

That single sweep, together with G8's coverage requirement, is the complete feasibility statement
for the deployment path — and it needs no new annotation.
