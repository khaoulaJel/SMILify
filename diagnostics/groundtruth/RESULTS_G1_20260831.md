# G1 — the first accuracy measurement: the fitted skeleton is 17% better than not fitting, and 8% on hard specimens

Fourteen human-annotated specimens (`annotation/gt_batch1/`, bench_17), all already fitted under
the production recipe in Z8. Zero new compute. Similarity-Procrustes aligned on the joints placed in
both; error reported as a percentage of each specimen's own mesosoma length (`b_t → b_a_3`).

**Every prior metric in this project measured precision.** `gen@20/spread` is population
self-consistency, trait `R` is nest-mate self-agreement, classification is a downstream proxy. This
is the first measurement of **accuracy**.

## The headline

| set | n | template | fitted | improvement | fit helps |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **all specimens** | 14 | 34.4% | **28.5%** | **17.0%** | 10/14 |
| original 10 (well-fitting) | 10 | 33.4% | 27.5% | 17.9% | 7/10 |
| **worst-quartile 4** | 4 | 34.8% | **32.2%** | **7.6%** | 3/4 |

**On four of fourteen the fit places joints worse than not fitting at all.** And the improvement
more than halves on the hardest specimens: the first ten annotated contained no specimen from the
worst-fitting quartile, so 17.9% was a best-case figure. On the four worst-quartile specimens
added since, fitting buys **7.6%**, and on *Eciton burchellii* it is actively worse (−1.5%).

The baseline is deliberately a strong one: the template is the population mean shape, which is
already a decent ant, and Procrustes gives it optimal scale and pose. "18% better than the mean
ant" is the honest reading — not "barely better than nothing", but far less than the pipeline's
self-consistency metrics imply.

## Where the fitting works, and where it does not

| segment | n | template | fitted | improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| coxa | 60 | 19.7% | **13.5%** | **31.6%** |
| tibia | 56 | 31.1% | 23.0% | 25.9% |
| trochanter | 60 | 22.2% | 17.4% | 21.4% |
| tarsus | 55 | 40.9% | 32.3% | 21.0% |
| pretarsus | 48 | 57.4% | **45.6%** | 20.6% |
| femur | 60 | 37.0% | 29.6% | 19.9% |
| body axis | 59 | 38.6% | 32.3% | 16.4% |
| **anterior** (head, mandibles, antennae) | 71 | 35.4% | **33.1%** | **6.6%** |

Proximal (coxa+trochanter+femur): 25.3% → 17.8%, a **29.5%** improvement.
Distal (tibia+tarsus+pretarsus): 41.1% → 35.4%, a **13.8%** improvement.

Two things fall out. **Distal joints are both the worst-placed and the least improved by fitting** —
pretarsi sit at 45.6% error after fitting. That is the direct confirmation of REPORT §4's claim
that the area-sampled data term cannot see them (tibia+tarsus+pretarsus = 2.4% of a leg chain's
area), measured against truth rather than inferred. **And the anterior is the least improved region
of the entire animal at 6.6%** — the fitter barely moves the head, mandibles and antennae off their
template positions.

## The head joint: a convention difference, not a defect

`b_h` is coincident with `b_t` in the model template, so `|b_t - b_h| = 0.0` on all 757 production
fits. The annotator placed the head joint at an offset instead: mean 14.3% of centroid size, range
**1.2%-49.5%**.

**That is a convention difference, and the model's convention is the correct one.** The shared
joint sits at x = 0.289, which is the thorax's forward edge (thorax spans -0.155 to 0.289) and just
inside where the head geometry begins (0.272) -- i.e. **at the neck**, the head-mesosoma
articulation. In a kinematic chain a child joint placed at its parent's position means the head is
attached at the neck and rotates there, which is anatomically right. A joint in this rig is a
ROTATION PIVOT, not a "where is the head" landmark.

The 40-fold spread in the annotated offsets (1.2% to 49.5%) is the signature of a landmark with no
agreed definition, and is itself the argument for the convention: **`b_h` should be annotated
coincident with `b_t`.**

`b_h` was excluded from the Procrustes alignment and from every error table above before any of
this was computed, precisely because it is degenerate on the fitted side, so no number in this
document depends on it. The "anterior" row is mandibles and antennae only.

**An earlier draft of this file claimed the coincident joint was a structural defect that explained
the §6.10 head ratio and Z2's failure. That was wrong and is withdrawn.** The §6.10 head defect
remains unexplained.

## An independent confirmation, for free

The annotator marked `w_1_r`, `w_2_r`, `w_1_l`, `w_2_l` as **"not present — biological" on all ten
specimens**, working from the meshes alone. That independently reproduces the Z1b finding that
these are wing joints carrying no geometry on a wingless worker, and confirms the `part_groups.py`
mislabel correction. A human and the skinning weights reached the same conclusion.

## What this reframes

The Z-series established that the registration is self-consistent at its noise floor and that
traits derived from it are unrepeatable. G1 says why: **the underlying skeleton is only marginally
more accurate than the population mean.** Measurements taken off it were never going to be
repeatable, the genus signal was never going to be strong, and no amount of work on the
optimiser's self-consistency was going to change that.

It also means the ceiling is *not* purely irreducible scan noise, as Z6 suggested on precision
grounds alone: a data term that cannot see the distal limbs is a fixable cause, which reopens a
door the precision-only analysis had closed.

## Limits of this result, stated plainly

- **n = 10**, one annotator, one pass.
- **No annotation-repeatability control.** `bench_10` and `bench_17` share four specimens, but only
  bench_17 was annotated, so no specimen has two independent annotations. Without that we cannot
  separate annotator uncertainty from fitter error — and distal tarsi and pretarsi, which carry the
  largest errors here, are exactly the joints hardest to place by hand. **The distal numbers in
  particular should be treated as an upper bound on fitter error until this control exists.**
- Procrustes with uniform scale is fitted on shared joints, so it absorbs global pose and scale
  disagreement; what remains is shape disagreement. That is the right comparison for morphometrics,
  and it is generous to the template baseline as well as to the fit.

## The single highest-value next step

**Re-annotate two or three specimens blind.** One hour of work converts every number above from a
comparison into a validated measurement, and it is the difference between "the fitter is 18% better
than the template" and "the fitter is 18% better than the template, against an annotation
uncertainty of X%".
