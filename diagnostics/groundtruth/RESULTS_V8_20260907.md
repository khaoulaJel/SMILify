# V8 — sparse surface observations DO reach unseen anatomy, and almost for free: held-out
# landmark error halves for 7% more chamfer. But they do not touch the skeleton.

Job 3838251, 5m17s, 12 annotated specimens. Loss is **point-to-surface** — `mean_p min_v ‖p−v‖²`,
assignment recomputed every step — because V7 showed a fixed template vertex cannot represent an
anatomical point across these species (held-out 44.2% of Weber's length against a 10.1%
nearest-surface floor; 77% of that error is the index, not the fit).

**Four landmarks are never supervised** — `mandibular_apex_l`, `antennal_insertion_l`,
`scape_apex_l`, `wl_posterior_r`, one per region, all left-side or rear — and the verdict is read
on those alone.

## The result

**Held-out landmark error 12.9% → 6.9%, a 47% reduction, for 1.07× chamfer.** Supervising eight
surface points pulls the surface toward four anatomical locations the objective never saw. That is
generalisation, not reachability — the supervised points go to 0.1%, which is the reachability
number and is not the claim.

**And it is nearly free.** 1.07× chamfer against the 1.54× that joint supervision costs for its
gain (V5b). The prediction that motivated the whole landmark direction — that surface observations
should avoid the structural cost of constraining an interior representation — holds.

## The other half: it does nothing for the skeleton

**Joint error is unchanged: 33.22% → 33.00%.** Not worse, not better. Sparse surface supervision
improves the surface and leaves the rig exactly where it was.

Set against V5b, the two interventions are **complementary and non-overlapping**:

| | anatomy it fixes | chamfer cost |
|---|---|---|
| joint supervision (λ=0.03) | joints 32.7% → 15.2% | 1.54× |
| **landmark supervision (λ=1)** | **held-out surface 12.9% → 6.9%** | **1.07×** |

Neither transfers to the other's endpoint. That is the strongest possible motivation for the
combined arm, and it is now a real question rather than a hedge.

## Limits

- **The floor is 10.1%** (V7, nearest-surface on the production fit) — yet held-out error reaches
  **6.9%**, *below* it. That is not a contradiction: V7's floor was measured on the production fit,
  and V8 moves the surface. But it does mean the supervised fit is now closer to the human
  landmarks than the production surface ever was, and the remaining 6.9% is approaching human
  placement variability, which is unmeasured. **A repeat-annotation of one specimen would bound
  it** and is the cheapest missing control in the project.
- **Bilateral asymmetry is noisy and possibly worse**: 0.074 → 0.143 at λ=1, but 0.071 at λ=10.
  At n = 12 this is not interpretable either way; it is flagged, not explained.
- λ=1 vs λ=0.3 vs λ=10 differ by less than the run-to-run noise this fitter shows (~0.01 on
  gen@20, Z4/Z5). Treat the operating point as "λ ≳ 0.3", not as 1.0 exactly.
- Three of the four held-out landmarks are on the left and one is at the rear; the split tests
  spatial generalisation, not generalisation to a different KIND of structure.
