# G3 — the model can place its joints at the truth, but only by leaving its own shape space

> **STATUS: the Case B verdict below is SUSPENDED.** G4 found that the oracle reported here drives
> `betas` to **|z| ~ 19** — nineteen prior standard deviations, against the production fit's
> |z| ~ 1.0 — and that the production objective's chamfer term at that solution is **0.623** against
> production's 0.00094, in a space normalised to extent ~1. The joints land; the mesh is absurd.
> An oracle outside the model's own prior does not demonstrate that the representation can express
> the anatomy, so "Case B confirmed" is withdrawn pending G6, which repeats this under a hard
> plausibility bound (`|z| <= 1, 2, 3`) and reports chamfer alongside the joint residual.
>
> The boundary this crossed was already stated in the "What this does NOT show" section below —
> that this tests joint positions rather than the mesh surface. What was not checked, and should
> have been, is whether the parameters reaching those joints were plausible. They were not.

14 human-annotated specimens. The model's joints are fitted **directly** to the annotated joints:
correspondence is not merely fixed but trivial — model joint *j* must reach annotated joint *j*.
No chamfer term, no candidate selection, no data association. Every mechanism previously suspected
of causing the residual is removed, leaving only "can these parameters put these joints there".

Residuals in the same units as G1: median % of the specimen's own mesosoma length.

## Result

| configuration | median | range |
|---|---:|---|
| unfitted template | 34.0% | — |
| **production D1_PROD fit** | **28.2%** | — |
| oracle — pose only (`global_rot`, `joint_rot`) | **6.3%** | 4.9–10.9% |
| oracle — **+ betas (25-D shape space)** | **3.7%** | 2.1–6.0% |
| oracle — + per-joint scale & translation | 0.0% | 0.0–0.2% |

**The honest headline is the 3.7% row.** With nothing but global rotation, joint rotations and the
25 betas — no per-joint scale, no per-joint translation, no free-form deformation — the model
places its joints **7.6× closer to the human annotations than the production pipeline does**.

**Case B (optimisation) is confirmed and Case A (representation) is excluded.** The parameterisation
is not the binding constraint. The objective is failing to find a solution that demonstrably exists
inside its own search space.

## The 0.0% row is real but uninformative, and should not be quoted

`log_beta_scales` and `betas_trans` supply 165 + 165 free parameters against roughly 140 target
coordinates (49 joints × 3, less the 7 degrees of freedom the similarity alignment absorbs). That
system is **underdetermined**, so reaching zero is close to arithmetic rather than evidence about
anatomy. It is reported for completeness and excluded from the conclusion.

The interesting structure is in the first two rows, and it is not a parameter count:

- **Pose alone plateaus at 6.3% and cannot go lower**, because rotations cannot change bone
  lengths — the distances between adjacent joints are fixed by the template. No amount of
  articulation reaches a specimen whose segment proportions differ from the mean ant.
- **Adding the 25 betas takes it to 3.7%**, because the shape space is exactly what varies those
  segment lengths. This is direct evidence that the shape space carries real, usable anatomical
  variation — the same conclusion the classifier work reached from the opposite direction.

## What this closes

**Stop modifying the model.** Adding degrees of freedom, changing the deformation parameterisation,
or redesigning the representation addresses a problem that is not there. Phase 3B is not the branch.

**Functional maps are excluded on this evidence, not deferred.** The conditional was: reopen them
if the error looks like correspondence error. Here correspondence was *trivially perfect* and the
model still reached 3.7% while the pipeline sits at 28.2%. The residual is not a correspondence
failure, and a better correspondence method cannot address an objective that ignores a solution it
can already reach.

**The attack surface is the objective**: priors, term weighting, constraints, initialisation, and
the optimiser's trajectory. That is a much smaller and better-specified space than "improve
correspondence" or "improve the model".

## What this does NOT show

- It tests whether the model can reach the **annotated joint positions**, not whether it can
  reproduce the full **mesh surface** (10,235 vertices vs 49 joints). Joint reachability is the
  directly relevant test — the pipeline's morphometrics are derived from joints and part
  dimensions — and the 3.7% vs 28.2% margin is far too large to be explained by that gap, but the
  distinction is real and should be stated rather than glossed.
- It does not identify *which* part of the objective is at fault. It establishes that the fault is
  there.
- Annotation error is still unmeasured (no blind re-annotation), so 3.7% is an upper bound on the
  oracle's true residual — which only strengthens the conclusion.

## The next experiment this licenses

The oracle produces, for every annotated specimen, a **parameter vector that is known to be close
to the truth**. That is a diagnostic instrument the project has never had:

> Evaluate the production objective at the oracle parameters and at the production parameters. If
> the objective scores the oracle **worse**, the objective is wrong — and the responsible term can
> be identified by decomposing the difference term by term (chamfer, edge, normal, laplacian,
> offset, symmetry, midline, limit, scale). If the objective scores the oracle **better**, the
> objective is right and the optimiser is not reaching it, which is an initialisation and
> trajectory problem instead.

That single comparison splits Case B into its two sub-cases and points directly at either the
term weighting or the search. It needs no new fitting — only forward evaluations of the existing
loss at two parameter settings.
