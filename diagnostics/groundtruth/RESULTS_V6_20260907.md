# V6 — the fitted surface is roughly right. The LANDMARKS were wrong. T1's mandible problem was
# a measurement artifact, not a fitting failure.

12 human-placed surface landmarks per specimen, on the 12 expert specimens. The first
**surface** accuracy measurement in this project — every earlier number was joint-based (against
interior rotation pivots) or self-referential (trait values against bounds we chose).

## The measurement, and the check that saved it

Distance from the fitted surface to the human landmark, in % of Weber's length:

| | median |
|---|---:|
| the template vertex we *call* that landmark | **48.8%** |
| the **nearest point on the same fitted surface** | **7.7%** |

**6.4×.** The surface passes close to every human landmark; the vertex indexed as that landmark
does not. Reported as-is, the first number would have read as "the fit is catastrophically wrong on
the surface". It is not — **the landmark definitions are.**

This was a stated risk, not a surprise: `LANDMARK_PROTOCOL` marks 5 of 10 template landmarks
`source="verify"`, and RESULTS_T4 says in its own limits that the advantage "could move once they
are corrected". V6 is that correction, and it moves.

Also settled in passing: production vs the template in the same pose (betas = 0, deform = 0) is
53.6% vs 52.8% — **the shape space and the deformation field buy nothing at all on these
landmarks**, because the indices are not measuring the anatomy they name.

## Recalibration from the human data

For each landmark, each of the 12 specimens votes for the fitted-mesh vertex nearest its human
landmark; the consensus is the medoid of those votes.

| landmark | n | agreement | moved from old |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| clypeal_ant_mid | 12 | 6.4% | 6.4% |
| cephalic_post_mid | 12 | 7.3% | 9.3% |
| mandibular_apex_r | 12 | 7.9% | **12.6%** |
| mandibular_apex_l | 12 | 7.9% | **11.9%** |
| antennal_insertion_r/l | 11 | 8.4 / 9.0% | 5.0 / 4.0% |
| scape_apex_r/l | 11 | **17.0 / 15.5%** | 14.6 / **20.7%** |
| wl_anterior_r / posterior_r | 12 | 8.9 / 7.9% | 7.8 / 5.4% |
| head_width_r/l | 12 | 11.0 / 8.4% | *(new — no old index)* |

*(both columns are % of the template diagonal, i.e. of body length)*

The old indices sat **4–21% of a body length** away from where the human data puts them.

## The consequence, tested on all 757 fits

| ratio | HUMAN truth | OLD indices | RECALIBRATED |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ML/HL | **0.35** | 0.95 | **0.40** |
| HW/HL | **0.88** | 1.10 | **0.72** |
| SL/HL | **1.08** | 1.05 | 1.25 |

| ratio | violations, OLD | violations, RECALIBRATED |
|---|---:|---:|
| **ML/HL** | **44** | **0** |
| SL/HL | 7 | 1 |
| HW/HL | 33 | 48 |
| **any** | **8.7%** | **6.5%** |

**ML/HL — the largest violation class, 44 of 87 — goes to ZERO**, and the corpus median moves from
0.95 to 0.40 against a human median of **0.35**. The old mandible landmark was inflating measured
mandible length by roughly 2.4×. That is not a fit that produces impossible mandibles; it is a
ruler that reads long.

## Why this matters beyond the number

**It retroactively explains a chain of failures.** T1 found 9.1% impossible traits and proposed a
mechanism; T2 refuted it. V1 proposed deformation; V2 refuted it on the full corpus. V3 proposed a
pose/shape identifiability degeneracy; it too came back null (p = 0.476). **Four attempts to find a
fitting cause for the mandible violations, all null — because for ML/HL there was no fitting cause
to find.** The violations were in the measurement.

**And it re-scopes V4.** The hinge drove violations 9.1% → 0.1% by pushing a mis-measured quantity
back inside a bound. It was patching a ruler.

## What this does NOT overturn

- **HW/HL got worse (33 → 48), and the comparison is confounded.** The old HW is a maximum lateral
  *extent* over the head part; the recalibrated one is a distance between two landmark vertices.
  Those are different definitions, and a two-vertex distance cannot capture a maximum. The extent
  form may well be the better estimator; only the ML/HL and SL/HL rows are like-for-like.
- **SL/HL got worse** (err 0.03 → 0.17). One of three moved the wrong way.
- **Agreement is 6–17% of body length**, so landmarks are recalibrated, not pinned. The scape apexes
  (17.0%, 15.5%) are the least reliable and should be treated as provisional.
- The 12 votes conflate correspondence error, human placement variability, and genuine anatomical
  differences between species. This does not separate them.
- **T4's 2.8× surface-over-joint advantage is NOT refuted** — it compared measurement *classes* on
  identical fits, and T4 flagged this exact risk itself. It should be recomputed on the
  recalibrated set before being quoted further.
- n = 12, and the same 12 specimens define the landmarks that are then applied to all 757.
  Independent validation needs specimens outside the calibration set.
