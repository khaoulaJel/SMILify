## The correction to V1, stated plainly

**V1's finding "deform_verts makes the impossible traits" is WITHDRAWN.** I generalised from the
wrong verb. What V1 actually showed, and what still stands, is that **the joint term with free
deformation CREATES new violations** (1 → 6 at λ=0.1, with deform RMS up 2.4×). What V1 did *not*
show, and what V2 refutes, is that deformation **causes** the violations already present in the
corpus.

The two are separable and I conflated them. V1's zero-deformation arms were **re-optimised** with
deformation held at zero — the whole solution moved — so their zero violations came from landing
somewhere else entirely, not from the absence of the deformation layer. V2 removes only the layer
and holds everything else fixed, which is the actual test of causation, and it comes back negative.

V1's n = 14 subset carried just **one** production violation, so it had almost no pre-existing
signal to explain. That should have capped the claim at the time.

**What survives from V1 unchanged:** the joint term inflates deformation and creates violations;
freezing deformation makes joints worse, so deformation is not what hides the skeleton (H3 there);
and `L01_dzero` remains the best arm found on that subset.

## Limits

- **Ablation, not re-optimisation.** This answers "did the deformation layer produce these values",
  not "what would a fitter without deformation produce". V1 answers the second, at n = 14.
- **My F-ratio is not T4's.** At min-per-genus = 8 the subset matches T4 exactly (27 genera, 386
  specimens) but mean F reads 0.30 against T4's 5.10, so the variance-ratio definitions differ.
  The WITH-vs-WITHOUT **ratio** is a valid within-V2 comparison and is what H3 rests on; the
  absolute values must not be quoted against T4. Reconciling the two definitions is an open item.
- Trait **validity**, never accuracy. No surface ground truth exists.
