# R7 — INCONCLUSIVE, and blocked upstream: a reliable anatomical midline could not be constructed
# on these scans. The laterality method was never actually tested.

Job 3865623 (and two earlier attempts). **This experiment does not have a result.** It is recorded
because the obstacle it hit is real, is upstream of the question it asked, and would block anyone
attempting the same thing.

## What was attempted

R6 showed the SDF appendage mask merges left and right structures, so the plan was
**attachment-based lateralisation**: walk each appendage vertex back through the mesh's own edge
graph to its nearest **body** vertex, and inherit the side of that attachment. This is deliberately
not a position-based split — a crossed mandible's *tip* is on the wrong side, but its *articulation*
never is, and V11b measured that crossing directly.

That method needs one thing: **a midline plane.** Three constructions were tried.

| midline from | result |
|---|---|
| the fitted body centroid | both head-width landmarks on the **same** side (+0.185, +0.106 on Formica) |
| midpoints of fitted bilateral **part** pairs | valid on **1 of 12** specimens |
| midpoints of **expert bilateral joint** pairs | valid on **2 of 11** specimens |

The validity test is minimal and non-negotiable: the two head-width landmarks are the widest points
left and right of the head, so they must fall on opposite sides of a correct midline. They do not.

**A built-in gate caught this.** Without it the run would have reported "0/12 mandibles correctly
lateralised" — a confident negative about a method that was never exercised.

## Why the midline fails, which is the actual finding

Bilateral joint pairs disagree about where the lateral axis points:

| specimen | pairs | median angle between a pair's axis and the mean | head-width test |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Aenictus | 20 | 12° | same side |
| Cataglyphis | 18 | 16° | same side |
| Cyphomyrmex | 22 | 20° | same side |
| Cephalotes | 19 | 24° | straddles |
| Aphaenogaster | 22 | 27° | same side |

**These are pinned museum specimens.** Legs are splayed at arbitrary asymmetric angles, so the
left–right axis derived from `l_2_co_r` disagrees with the one from `l_3_ti_l` by 12–27°. Averaging
them produces a plane that is not the anatomical midline, and the offset is enough to put both head
sides on one side of it.

> **The anatomical midline of a pinned ant scan is not recoverable by averaging bilateral pairs —
> neither from the fit nor from expert joint annotations.** It is its own estimation problem,
> upstream of laterality, and it has to be solved before attachment-based splitting can even be
> evaluated.

That is consistent with everything else the project has found about this frame: qc_annotations
flagged many apparent laterality violations that turned out to be posture, and R2/R4 showed the
fit's own parts are misplaced.

## What would be tried next

Estimate the midline from the **scan's own reflective symmetry** — find the plane minimising the
distance between the mesh and its own mirror image — rather than from any landmark or joint set.
The body is bilaterally symmetric even when the legs are splayed, so a symmetry-plane fit weighted
toward the thick (body) region should be robust to exactly the posture that defeats pair-averaging.
That is a standard, cheap construction and it does not depend on the fit.

**Until then R7's question — does attachment-based lateralisation work? — remains open and untested.**
