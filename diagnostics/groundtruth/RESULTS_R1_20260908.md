# R1 — regional constraints are validated, but not for the reason expected: exact vertex identity
# is *reachable*, it is just **expensive in surface fidelity**. Region ≈5% of body length is free.

Job 3856419, 4m59s, 12 annotated specimens. All 12 landmarks supervised, all 12 measured — a
**reachability** test by design, which is the correct form for *"can the representation consume this
constraint"*. It makes no generalisation claim; V13 showed those do not hold at this sample size.

    L = mean_p  min over v in REGION(p)  || p − v ||²

Region radius interpolates continuously between the two formulations already measured: radius 0 is
a single fixed vertex, radius ∞ is V12's S12.

| region radius | vertices | landmark error | **chamfer** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| production | — | 10.1% | 1.00× |
| **0.0%** (a single vertex) | 1 | **0.1%** | **1.37×** |
| 0.5% | 3 | 0.2% | 1.35× |
| 1.0% | 13 | 0.2% | 1.33× |
| 2.0% | 50 | 0.1% | 1.27× |
| **5.0%** | 337 | **0.1%** | **1.18×** |
| 10.0% | 1763 | 0.1% | 1.12× |
| ∞ (= S12) | all | 0.2% | 1.14× |

## The result is in the chamfer column, not the error column

**Every granularity reaches the landmarks.** Anatomical accuracy is ~0.1–0.2% at every radius,
including a single fixed vertex. My script's verdict line — *"the smallest region reaching <1% is
0.0%"* — is literally true and misses the point entirely. It was written expecting exact-vertex
supervision to fail on accuracy. It does not.

**What changes monotonically is the cost in surface fidelity.** Forcing one designated vertex to sit
on the landmark costs **1.37× chamfer**; letting any vertex of a ~5%-of-body-length region do it
costs **1.18×**, and the cost saturates there — 10% and unrestricted are 1.12× and 1.14×.

> **Exact vertex identity buys nothing and costs ~20% additional surface distortion.**

That is a mechanism, not just a preference. If template vertex 6125 is not anatomically the mandible
tip on *this* species — and V7 showed it is not, across species — then dragging it there deforms the
surface around it. Allowing a region to satisfy the constraint lets the anatomically correct piece
of surface do the work, and the mesh is not distorted to compensate.

## What this validates, and what it specifies

**The abstraction is validated.** Region-level supervision achieves identical anatomical accuracy to
free assignment at essentially identical surface cost, while carrying semantic meaning that free
assignment does not. A semantic layer that outputs *"this scan region is the left scape"* is a
sufficient interface for the fitter.

**And it gives a quantitative specification for that layer:**

> **Localisation to ≈5% of the template diagonal (~337 vertices) captures the full benefit.**
> Below that the surface cost rises; above it nothing further is gained.

An automatic anatomical extractor does **not** need vertex-level precision. It needs to place each
anatomical structure within ~5% of body length — which is roughly "the right part, roughly the right
place along it", and is far weaker than what W3/W5 were asked to deliver.

## Limits

- **Reachability, not generalisation.** All 12 landmarks are supervised. R1 shows the fitter *can*
  consume regional constraints; V13 already showed that supervising a subset does not reliably fix
  the rest, and R1 does not revisit that.
- Regions are Euclidean balls on the template around V6's recalibrated consensus vertex. A true
  anatomical region would be geodesic and part-bounded; at 5% of the diagonal a Euclidean ball can
  cross between adjacent structures (e.g. from a scape onto the head), which would make this
  measurement **optimistic** for the tight radii and roughly correct for the loose ones.
- n = 12, one run per radius, one λ. Differences under ~0.05× in chamfer are not readable.
- The 1.12× at radius 10% versus 1.14× at ∞ is noise, not evidence that a bounded region beats an
  unbounded one.
