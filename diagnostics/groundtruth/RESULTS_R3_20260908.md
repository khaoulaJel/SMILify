# R3 — the named parts CAN reach the anatomy, but only if the supervision names them.
# Point-to-surface supervision leaves the model's mandible exactly where it was.

Job 3857580, 2m25s, 12 annotated specimens. Three arms, **identical targets**, differing only in
which vertices are permitted to satisfy them.

| arm | **err to the CORRECT part** | err to any surface | chamfer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| production | **35.5%** | 10.1% | 1.00× |
| **CORRECT** (constrained to the anatomically right part) | **0.2%** | 0.2% | **1.25×** |
| CONSENSUS (R2's mostly-head assignment) | 27.4% | 0.2% | 1.17× |
| FREE (= V12's S12, any vertex) | **34.7%** | 0.2% | 1.10× |

## The result

**All three supervised arms reach the landmarks equally well — 0.2% to *some* surface.** They are
indistinguishable on the metric the entire V-series used.

**They are not remotely equivalent anatomically.** Supervising freely leaves the named structures
**34.7%** of a body length from the anatomy — essentially unchanged from production's 35.5%. Only
the arm that *names the part* relocates them, to **0.2%**.

> **Point-to-surface supervision satisfies a landmark by moving whatever surface happens to be
> nearby. The model's mandible ends up exactly as misplaced as it started.**

## What this settles

**1. The template is not the limitation.** The named parts *can* be placed on the real anatomy —
mandible, scape, antennal insertion, all to 0.2% — using pose, shape and deformation exactly as
production uses them, for **1.25× chamfer** against free supervision's 1.10×. Fourteen percent more
surface cost buys correctly-placed anatomy. **Do not rebuild the model.**

**2. The objective is the limitation, and specifically its lack of semantics.** Nothing in the
production objective, and nothing in a point-to-surface landmark term, ever asks a *named* structure
to go anywhere. Both are satisfied by any nearby surface. This is the surface-side analogue of G6's
finding that no term constrains joint *position* — here, no term constrains part *identity*.

**3. Semantic supervision is not an optimisation; it is the missing information.** The difference
between FREE (34.7%) and CORRECT (0.2%) is not a weight, a schedule or a better optimiser. It is
one bit per landmark: *which part should reach this point.* That is exactly what an automatic
anatomical layer would supply, and R3 measures what it is worth.

## What it means for every surface number in the V-series

V6–V13 measured **point-to-surface** distance throughout. R3 shows that metric cannot distinguish a
fit whose anatomy is correct from one whose anatomy is 35% of a body length wrong — both read 0.2%.

**Those numbers are correct as stated and measure surface proximity, not anatomical correctness.**
Any future evaluation must report the distance to the *named* part alongside, or it will certify
fits that are anatomically wrong. This is now the single most important measurement convention in
the project.

## Limits

- n = 12, one run per arm. The 1.25× vs 1.10× chamfer difference is small but consistent with the
  extra constraint being real work.
- Part membership is by dominant skinning weight, and `an_3_r`/`an_3_l` carry only 4 vertices each,
  so distal antennal segments are barely represented in the template regardless of fitting.
- Reachability, not generalisation: all 12 landmarks are supervised. R3 shows the named parts *can*
  be relocated when told to; it does not show a fit would find them unaided.
- The correct-part assignment is anatomical judgement (`scape_apex → an_1 ∪ an_2`, etc.), not
  derived. A different reasonable assignment could shift the numbers, though not by the margin that
  separates 0.2% from 34.7%.
