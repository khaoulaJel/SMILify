## The finding is in the assignment table, not the error column

Each landmark was assigned to the part containing its V6 recalibrated consensus vertex. **Ten of
twelve landed in `b_h`, the head** — including both mandibular apexes and both antennal insertions.
`scape_apex_r` landed in `ma_r`, the *mandible*.

The **original September indices were in the correct parts** — `mandibular_apex_r` → `ma_r`,
`scape_apex_r` → `an_1_r`. **V6's recalibration moved them out.**

So I measured how far the human landmarks are from the model's own correctly-named parts:

| landmark | named part | **distance to that part** | distance to nearest surface |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| mandibular_apex_r | `ma_r` | **50.4%** | 12.1% |
| mandibular_apex_l | `ma_l` | **30.0%** | 14.9% |
| scape_apex_r | `an_1_r` | **73.6%** | 20.8% |
| scape_apex_l | `an_1_l` | **80.7%** | 23.6% |
| antennal_insertion_r | `an_1_r` | **42.7%** | 4.2% |
| antennal_insertion_l | `an_1_l` | **27.2%** | 4.3% |

*(% of Weber's length, production fits. The parts exist and are populated: `ma_r` 195 verts,
`an_1_r` 71.)*

> **The fitted model's mandible is not where the real mandible is. Its antenna is not where the real
> antenna is. By 27–81% of a body length.**

## What this corrects

**1. V6's recalibration absorbed fit error.** It chose, per landmark, the fitted vertex *nearest* the
human point. Where the model's mandible is 50% of a body length off, the nearest vertex is a **head**
vertex. So the recalibrated indices reproduce the right *distances* — ML/HL 0.40 against a human 0.35
— while sitting on the **wrong structures**. V6's headline (44 mandible violations → 0) stands as a
statement about the *measurement*, but it was achieved partly by relocating the ruler onto stable
head geometry, not by finding the true mandible tip. **That was not visible in V6 and I did not
flag it.**

**2. R1 and R2 tested looser regions than they appear to.** R1's balls were centred on those
recalibrated (mostly head) vertices; R2's parts were mostly `b_h`, 21% of the mesh. So neither tested
whether *anatomically correct* parts suffice — they tested whether a large region near the head
suffices. **R2's verdict line — "part identity is a sufficient interface" — is not supported by this
run** and is withdrawn.

**3. The point-to-surface metric is blind to this, by construction.** It asks *"is some surface near
the landmark?"* — yes, 4–24%. It cannot ask *"is the **right** surface near the landmark?"* — and
there the answer is no, 27–81%. **Every surface-accuracy number in the V-series (V6–V13) inherits
this blindness.** They are correct as stated and measure less than the words suggest.

## What it means for the direction

This is the strongest evidence yet **for** the semantic-anatomy direction, and it makes the problem
concrete rather than architectural:

> The registration places *a* surface near the anatomy while placing the *named* structures far from
> it. That is exactly a semantic correspondence failure, and it is now measured: **27–81% of Weber's
> length on the anterior structures.**

It also explains an old puzzle. §6.10, X1, Z2 and the whole anterior thread searched for why the head
region misbehaves. **The mandibles and antennae of the fitted model are not on the real mandibles and
antennae.** A trait computed from them was never measuring the structure it names.

## Limits

- n = 12, production fits only. Whether a supervised fit relocates the parts correctly is **not**
  tested here and is the obvious next question.
- Part membership is by dominant skinning weight; a vertex on a boundary could be assigned either
  way. The magnitudes here (27–81%) are far too large for that to explain them.
- `an_3_r`/`an_3_l` have only 4 vertices each, so distal antennal parts are barely represented in the
  model at all — a separate limitation of the template.
