# inside its own prior. The ~7% is an inference gap, and correspondence identity is not the lever.

Job 3854166, 2m41s, 12 annotated specimens. The last diagnostic of the V-series.

## B — CAPACITY: refuted as the bottleneck

**S12 places all twelve landmarks at 0.2% of Weber's length, simultaneously, for 1.09× chamfer.**
Production is 10.1%.

The 25-PC shape space, pose and deformation — used exactly as production uses them, with no oracle
leaving the prior — **can represent every annotated anatomical location at once, essentially
exactly, at almost no cost in surface fit.**

This is the honest capacity test that G3's withdrawn 3.7% was not. G3 reached its number with betas
at |z| ≈ 19 and chamfer 0.623 against production's 0.00094 — the joints landed and the mesh was not
an ant. **S12 stays inside the model's normal parameterisation throughout.**

> **The branch point resolves: YES, the model can represent the anatomy under plausible parameters.
> Therefore make the anatomy automatic — do not rebuild the representation.**

## A — CORRESPONDENCE IDENTITY is not the lever

`S8_ORACLE` supervises the per-specimen vertex that was genuinely nearest the human landmark in the
production fit — the correct correspondence identity, handed to the optimiser for free. It does
**not** help: **7.7% vs S8's 7.3%**, marginally worse.

Telling the optimiser *which vertex* should reach the landmark buys nothing over letting it choose
freely. Whatever the residual is, it is not the loss picking the wrong surface point.

**Correspondence does drift, mildly.** Tracking the production-correct vertex on the held-out
landmarks: production 12.9% → S8 **11.1%**, while nearest-surface goes 12.9% → **7.3%**. The surface
moves much closer to the landmark than that particular vertex does, so a *different* vertex ends up
nearest. Real, but small, and it is a consequence rather than the cause.

## What the ~7% actually is: an INFERENCE gap, and it is a coverage statement

Capacity is fine (S12) and correspondence identity is not the constraint (S8_ORACLE). What remains
is that **supervising 8 landmarks does not determine where the other 4 belong.** The shape space
does not couple them tightly enough for two thirds of the set to imply the rest, and chamfer does
not supply the difference.

**This unifies with G8.** G8 found joint supervision does not propagate to unsupervised joints and
needs ~75% coverage before the benefit becomes substantial. V12 supervises 8 of 12 landmarks — 67%
coverage — and leaves 7.3% on the remainder. **The same coverage requirement appears on the surface
channel as on the skeletal one.**

**Specification for whatever supplies the anatomy automatically:** *dense* coverage, not a few
well-chosen landmarks. Sparse anatomical information corrects what it touches and little else — on
both channels.

## Limits

- n = 12, one run per arm, no seed replication. The S8 vs S8_ORACLE difference (7.3 vs 7.7) is
  within noise and is reported as "no effect", not as oracle correspondence being worse.
- S12 supervises everything it measures, so 0.2% is a **reachability** figure by construction. That
  is exactly what a capacity test should be — it asks whether the parameterisation *can* hold the
  configuration, not whether it can find it unaided.
- One λ (=1) and one point-to-surface formulation throughout.
- Capacity is established for these 12 landmarks on these 12 specimens. A denser or more extreme
  set could still exceed the shape space.
