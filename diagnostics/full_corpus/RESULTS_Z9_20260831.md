# Z9 — the genus signal is statistically real and far too weak to use per specimen

757 workers, production fits, lot-blind protocol reused from `analyse.py` (`loo_1nn`,
`perm_test`, `accession_lot`) rather than reimplemented. 602 specimens across 83 genera are
lot-blind classifiable. Zero new compute.

## Test 1 — do two nest-mates get the same genus?

| | |
|---|---:|
| conspecific pairs (determined species, lot-blind) | 318 |
| **both nest-mates assigned the SAME genus** | **5.7%** |
| chance agreement (label permutation) | 3.0% ± 1.0 |
| lift | 1.89× |
| **both assigned the CORRECT genus** | **1.9%** |

**Two workers from the same colony receive the same genus 5.7% of the time.** This is
repeatability measured on the *output* rather than the inputs, and it is the number that decides
usability: a method that puts two nest-mates in different genera is not usable per specimen,
whatever its aggregate accuracy looks like.

Agreement and correctness are reported separately on purpose — a pair can agree and both be wrong.

## Test 2 — does dropping unreliable traits help?

Lot-blind LOO-1NN genus accuracy, permutation null over 200 draws:

| feature set | traits | accuracy | null | lift | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all traits | 71 | 0.091 | 0.022 | 4.13 | <0.0001 |
| R ≥ 0.0 | 60 | 0.095 | 0.022 | 4.28 | <0.0001 |
| **R ≥ 0.1** | **45** | **0.113** | 0.022 | **5.24** | <0.0001 |
| R ≥ 0.2 (drop all "noise") | 22 | 0.090 | 0.022 | 4.00 | <0.0001 |
| R ≥ 0.3 | 6 | 0.071 | 0.023 | 3.09 | <0.0001 |

**This qualifies Z8's reading, and my own recommendation from it.** Filtering by reliability helps
only mildly and only mildly applied: R ≥ 0.1 is the optimum at 5.24× against 4.13× for everything.
Filtering harder *hurts* — the 6 traits with R ≥ 0.3 give 3.09×, worse than using all 71.

So the individually-unrepeatable traits **do** carry pooled signal. "Drop the noise traits" was too
strong; the correct statement is that a mild reliability filter (R ≥ 0.1, 45 of 71 traits) is the
best feature set measured here.

## What is actually true about the genus result

**The signal is real.** 4–5× lift at p < 0.0001 under lot-blind validation, on 83 genera. It is not
an artifact of near-replicates: lot-blinding removes the specimens that share a colony and scan
session.

**The signal is small in absolute terms.** 9–11% accuracy. Against a 2.2% null that is a strong
statistical effect and a weak practical one.

**It does not support per-specimen use.** 5.7% nest-mate agreement and 1.9% both-correct mean the
method cannot identify an individual worker's genus. It supports population-level statements —
"worker shape carries genus information at 4–5× chance" — and nothing finer.

This is consistent with everything upstream rather than a new surprise: the registration is at its
noise floor (Z6), individual traits are unrepeatable (Z8, median R 0.149), and a classifier built
on them recovers a real but small pooled signal.

## What should be claimed

- **Report the pooled effect with its lift and null.** It is defensible.
- **Do not report per-specimen genus assignments**, or any claim whose unit is one specimen.
- **Use R ≥ 0.1 as the feature set** (45 traits) rather than all 71 or an aggressive filter.
- **Attach the conspecific-agreement figure to any classification claim.** It is the honest
  companion to accuracy and it is cheap to compute now that the paired structure is identified.

## The one open lead worth taking

`analyse.py`'s docstring is explicit that 1-NN was chosen because *"with ~60–150 specimens spread
over dozens of genera, most classifiers cannot be fit honestly"*. **That constraint no longer
holds — there are now 757 fitted specimens.** So 9–11% is a floor on achievable accuracy under a
deliberately weak classifier, not a ceiling on the information present.

The next test is therefore a properly cross-validated stronger classifier (regularised LDA or
similar) under the same lot-blind protocol and the same permutation null, on the R ≥ 0.1 feature
set. If it moves accuracy materially, the limit is the classifier and the morphometrics are more
usable than this reads. If it does not, the limit is the measurement, and Z6–Z9 have found the
ceiling of this pipeline.

That is a cheap test and it is the last one that can change the conclusion.
