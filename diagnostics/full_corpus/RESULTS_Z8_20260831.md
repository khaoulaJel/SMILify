# Z8 — at full power, no morphometric trait is repeatable between conspecific workers

All 757 workers, **production recipe** (`run_m1_fit_all.sh`: hierarchical H0–H2 init → moonshot
with `D1_PROD.yaml`) — the chain the morphometrics report is actually built on. Job 3354012,
1 h 58 m, COMPLETED, 12/12 chunks. 159 determined species with ≥2 specimens, 387 specimens,
**359 within-species pairs**.

`R = 1 − σ²_within-species / σ²_total`, species weighted equally.

| | count |
|---|---:|
| **R ≥ 0.5 — usable** | **0** |
| 0.2 ≤ R < 0.5 — weak | 22 |
| **R < 0.2 — noise** | **49** (11 negative) |
| **median R** | **0.149** |

Best traits: `shapedev_mesosoma` 0.458, `gaster_len` 0.407, `gaster_wid` 0.373, `seg_an_2_r` 0.350,
`attach_b_a_1` 0.323, `mesosoma_len` 0.305. **Nothing clears 0.5.**

## Z7's usable set does not survive, and the reason is sample size

Z7 reported 8 usable traits from 25 pairs. Those estimates were optimistic, and I isolated why.

**The recipe is not the driver.** Same 50 specimens, same 25 pairs, log-shape-ratios recomputed on
the same 50 — like-for-like:

| | diagnostic recipe | production recipe |
|---|---:|---:|
| median within-pair sd | 0.2737 | 0.2453 |
| median total sd | 0.3211 | 0.2761 |
| **median R** | **0.217** | **0.224** |

Median R is essentially identical, and production is marginally *less* noisy. My first comparison
appeared to show a large recipe effect only because the total-variance denominator came from 757
specimens in one arm and 50 in the other.

**Individual R estimates at 25 pairs are unstable.** On identical specimens, switching recipe moves
single traits by up to 0.5:

| trait | diagnostic | production |
|---|---:|---:|
| `mandible_r_len` | 0.613 | **0.056** |
| `shapedev_gaster` | 0.559 | **0.012** |
| `shapedev_mesosoma` | 0.607 | 0.164 |
| `head_wid` | 0.691 | 0.489 |

The *median* is stable across recipes; any *individual* R from 25 pairs is not. **Z7's named
usable set should not be used.** The 359-pair full-corpus table is the estimate to work from.

## What this means for the morphometrics

**The aggregate genus result is not contradicted.** 1-NN genus classification at 6.1× lift
(p < 0.0001) is multivariate, and a classifier can extract real signal from many individually weak
features. A median R of 0.149 across 71 traits is entirely compatible with a strong pooled signal —
it is what a distributed signal looks like when read one trait at a time.

**Single-trait biological claims are not supported at this noise level.** The cephalic-index result
(§4, *Odontomachus* 0.83 vs 0.98, p = 0.024) rests on `head_wid` (R = 0.227 at full power, the
strongest length trait) over `head_len` (0.132). Neither is repeatable between nest-mates. That
claim needs re-examination, not because the biology is wrong but because the measurement cannot
currently support a per-trait inference.

**Leg and antenna segment lengths are noise, confirmed at power.** `seg_l_2_co_r` −0.107,
`seg_l_1_ti_r` −0.034, `seg_l_2_ti_r` −0.018 — two nest-mates disagree as much as or more than two
random ants. The bilateral check compounds it: `seg_l_2_ti` reads −0.018 on the right and 0.205 on
the left, a 0.223 gap, so neither side is trustworthy. These are exactly the distal structures
REPORT §4 identified as invisible to the area-sampled data term, and exactly what the W- and
C-series spent its effort trying to recover. **They were never carrying signal.**

## Where the investigation stands

Seven fitter hypotheses closed, the registration shown to sit at its noise floor (Z6: a nest-mate
buys +0.0105, and conspecifics are only 7% closer than random ants), and now the measurement layer
quantified: at full power, per-trait repeatability is 0.149 median with nothing above 0.5.

The two facts fit together. The registration is at its floor *and* the traits derived from it are
individually unrepeatable — the second is the downstream consequence of the first. No further
fitter work will change either.

**The honest recommendation is to change what is claimed, not to keep optimising.** Report
multivariate results, which are supported. Do not report single-trait comparisons between taxa
without a repeatability figure beside them. And treat the R table as a required column in any
future morphometric claim, the way the proxy/mechanism rule became required for interventions.
