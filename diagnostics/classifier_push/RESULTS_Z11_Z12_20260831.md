# Z11 / Z12 — the classifier axis, pushed: top-1 0.113 → 0.179, nest-mate agreement 5.7% → 18.9%

757 workers, production fits, lot-blind leave-one-lot-out on 83 genera. Zero new fitting.

## The finding: betas and traits are complementary

`analyse.features()` has always used measurements taken off the fitted mesh. `measure_run` also
stores `betas` on every row, and no classifier had ever seen them — even though Z4 measured that
channel as the only transferable one the pipeline produces (gen@20/spread **0.2936** against
`deform_verts`' 0.9885).

Stage 1, LDA shrinkage, lot-blind top-1:

| block | dims | top-1 | top-3 | top-5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| traits R ≥ 0.1 | 45 | 0.145 | 0.281 | 0.341 |
| betas | 25 | 0.120 | 0.234 | 0.319 |
| log_beta_scales | 165 | 0.121 | 0.239 | 0.327 |
| **traits + betas** | **70** | **0.179** | **0.304** | **0.394** |
| traits + betas + scales | 235 | 0.166 | 0.314 | 0.395 |

**Betas do not replace the traits — they complement them.** Alone they are *worse* (0.120 vs
0.145); together they beat either by 3.4 points, and the effect held across all three model
families tested. The mechanism is visible in what we already measured: the traits are derived from
the fitted mesh and carry the free-form field's noise (Z8, median trait R 0.149), the betas are the
parametric channel and carry none of it. Two different error structures, so pooling helps.

**`log_beta_scales` adds nothing** — 235 features do worse on top-1 than 70. Consistent with Z3,
which found that channel unmodelled and non-transferring.

## The size control, which the result required

61% of the Mosimann log-size variable is predictable from the betas (R² = 0.612), and absolute size
is *not* recoverable from worker scans (`measure.py` header: meshes are centred and divided by
max|coord|). So a T+B gain could have been scan-scale leakage rather than shape.

| features | top-1 | top-3 | top-5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| traits + betas, as-is | **0.179** | 0.304 | 0.394 |
| **traits + betas, size-residualised** | **0.179** | 0.296 | 0.382 |

**Top-1 is identical with every trace of linear size dependence removed from the betas.** The gain
is shape, not scan scale. Residualising even slightly improves the betas alone (0.128 vs 0.120),
consistent with the size component being arbitrary noise.

## Stage 2 — tuned, with per-model nulls

C tuned by inner GroupKFold over the **training lots only**; modal C = 1.0 unbalanced, 3.0 balanced.

| model | top-1 | null | lift | top-3 | top-5 | macro-recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **LDA shrinkage** | **0.179** | 0.028 | 6.47× | **0.304** | **0.394** | 0.079 |
| logistic L2 (C=1.0) | 0.176 | 0.029 | 6.09× | 0.292 | 0.372 | 0.076 |
| logistic L2 balanced (C=3.0) | 0.163 | 0.017 | **9.66×** | 0.279 | 0.367 | 0.075 |

**Lift is not comparable across models and must not be quoted as an improvement.** The balanced
model has the highest lift in the table (9.66×) and the *lowest* accuracy, because its own null is
far lower. Z10's 7.21× likewise exceeds LDA's 6.47× while scoring 0.163 against 0.179. Accuracy is
the comparable number; lift only says a given model beats its own chance baseline.

**`class_weight="balanced"` fails on its own terms here.** It costs 1.6 points of top-1 and does
*not* improve macro-recall (0.075 vs LDA's 0.079) — the metric it exists to serve. Dropped.

## Usability, recomputed under the winner

| | 1-NN (as shipped) | Z10 logistic, traits | **Z12 LDA, traits+betas** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| top-1 accuracy | 0.113 | 0.163 | **0.179** |
| top-5 accuracy | — | — | **0.394** |
| nest-mates given the same genus | 5.7% | 10.7% | **18.9%** |
| both correct | 1.9% | 5.7% | **11.9%** |

Against the shipped 1-NN baseline: **+59% top-1, and nest-mate agreement 3.3×.**

## What this changes, and what it does not

**Changes.** The genus result is materially better than anything previously reported, and the
improvement is entirely on the analysis side — no refitting, no model change, no new data. Top-5 at
**0.394** is the number that matters for the realistic use: narrowing 83 genera to a shortlist for
a specialist. At roughly two in five, that is a usable assistive tool.

**Does not change.** 18.9% nest-mate agreement is still not per-specimen identification — two
workers from one colony get different answers four times in five. Every conclusion from Z1–Z10
stands: the registration is at its noise floor, no trait is individually repeatable, and per-trait
biological claims remain unsupported.

## The sample-size constraint on all of this

602 specimens sit in only **34 accession lots**, so lot-blind validation has an effective n of 34.
Differences of one or two points between configurations are not resolvable — LDA at 0.179 and
logistic at 0.176 are a tie. What *is* resolvable is the block-level effect: traits+betas beating
either alone by 3.4 points, consistently across three model families.

## Recommended configuration

**LDA with automatic shrinkage on traits (R ≥ 0.1) + betas, 70 features, lot-blind.** Report top-1
and top-5, accuracy rather than lift when comparing to other models, macro-recall alongside, and
conspecific agreement beside any classification claim.
